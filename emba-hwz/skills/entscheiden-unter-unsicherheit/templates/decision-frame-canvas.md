# Decision Frame Canvas

A one-page diagnostic to run before any consequential decision. The goal is to surface what is actually being decided and to size the rigor appropriately.

## 1. The decision in one sentence

> _"By [date], [decision-maker] will decide whether/which/how [action]."_

If this sentence is fuzzy, stop here. The decision is not yet ready.

## 2. The decision-maker(s)

> _Who actually has the authority to make this call? Name them. If the answer is "it depends," resolve that first._

## 3. The unit of analysis

> _Project, product, business unit, company, ecosystem? What is the boundary inside which we are reasoning?_

## 4. The no-decision baseline

> _What happens if we do not decide / do nothing? Describe the world in 6 months under the status-quo path._

Many "decisions" disappear when this question is answered honestly.

## 5. Reversibility

- [ ] **Type 1 (one-way door)** — Hard, slow, or expensive to reverse. Examples: an acquisition; a public commitment; a key hire at the top.
- [ ] **Type 2 (two-way door)** — Cheap and quick to reverse. Examples: a pricing test; a campaign; a vendor trial.

Match rigor to reversibility. Type 2 decisions over-engineered are a tax. Type 1 decisions under-engineered are catastrophic.

## 7. Time pressure

> _What is the real deadline (vs. the artificial deadline)? Who imposes it? What is the cost of waiting one more week to decide better?_

Most executives' decisions feel more urgent than they are. Some are genuinely time-critical.
