# Habit Architecture for Self-Leadership Practice

Personal practice is fragile against the pull of daily work. Without deliberate habit architecture, it doesn't survive a busy week. Use this canvas to design for sustainability.

---

## 1. The practice

**What practice are you committing to?**
> _Be specific: e.g., "10 minutes mindful breathing daily."_

**Minimum viable version** (the version you will do on the worst day):
> _e.g., "3 mindful breaths before stepping out of bed."_

## 2. Time and place

**Time of day:**
> _Same time each day. Morning is most reliable for most people._

**Specific location:**
> _Same place. The brain associates the place with the practice._

## 3. Trigger

**What cue starts the practice?**
> _Coffee brewing? Alarm? After teeth brushing? After morning walk? Tie to existing behavior._

## 4. Tiny entry

**The smallest version you can do in 90 seconds:**
> _Sit down. Three breaths. Done. The point is showing up, not duration._

The discipline is showing up. The duration grows naturally once showing up is reliable.

## 5. Accountability

**External support:**
- [ ] Practice partner — share with whom, what cadence
- [ ] Group — local sangha, online community, MBSR class
- [ ] App — Headspace, Calm, Insight Timer, Waking Up
- [ ] Journal — short daily entry
- [ ] Coach / therapist

## 6. Recovery from breaks

**The rule:** every practitioner misses. The return is the practice.

**My plan when I miss a day:**
- Don't catastrophize
- Don't try to "make up" the missed day
- Simply do the practice today
- Notice what got in the way without judgment

**My plan when I miss a week:**
- Restart with the minimum viable version tomorrow
- Don't aim for full practice immediately
- Build back over 3–5 days

## 7. When and why the practice will be hardest

_Anticipate the failure modes so they don't surprise you._

- **Travel:** plan how the practice survives travel (apps, ear buds, shorter version)
- **Crisis weeks:** the practice often abandons exactly when it would help most. Plan the minimum viable version explicitly.
- **After several weeks of consistency:** sometimes the practice becomes routine and loses meaning. Refresh by changing the practice slightly or attending a retreat.
- **When you don't feel like it:** the day you don't feel like it is often the day the practice matters most. Show up anyway.

## 8. Markers of progress

_How will you know it is working? Not over weeks but over months._

- **8-week markers:** _e.g., I have practiced 50 of 56 days. I notice the gap between trigger and reaction has widened._
- **3-month markers:** _e.g., Practice feels established. Others have commented on changes._
- **6-month markers:** _e.g., Capacity for difficult conversations has deepened. Sleep is better._
- **12-month markers:** _e.g., The practice is woven into who I am. I cannot imagine not having it._

## 9. Periodic refresh

The practice deepens with refresh:

- **Quarterly:** review what is working, what is not. Adjust.
- **Twice yearly:** day-long mini-retreat
- **Annually:** longer retreat (3–7 days silent retreat is a common rhythm for serious practitioners)

## 10. The commitment

_The practice is not the practice; the showing up is. Sign here for yourself._

> _I will practice [practice] at [time] in [place], starting [date]._
>
> _When I miss, I will return without drama. When it gets hard, I will reach for the minimum viable version. I will review progress at the 8-week mark._

This commitment is to yourself. It works in proportion to your honesty about it.
