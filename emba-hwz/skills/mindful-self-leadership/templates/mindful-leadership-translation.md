# Mindful Leadership Translation Canvas

Personal practice produces capacity. The translation to leadership behavior is where the capacity actually shows up. Use to design specific protocols for the leadership situations that matter most.

---

## 1. Before high-stakes meetings

**Protocol:**
- _2 minutes alone before entering — even if it means rearranging time_
- _3 physiological sighs to reset autonomic state_
- _Set intention: "What kind of leader do I want to be in this meeting?"_
- _Surface what is at stake for me — fears, hopes, identity threats_
- _Enter present, not running on autopilot_

## 2. In conflict

**Protocol:**
- _Notice the trigger — body sensation usually faster than thought_
- _Pause. Internal naming: "I'm noticing irritation / fear / impatience"_
- _Physiological sigh if needed_
- _Ask: "What does this situation actually call for?"_
- _Choose response based on what serves, not on what would feel good_

## 3. In feedback conversations (giving)

**Protocol:**
- _Before: clarify intent — am I trying to help or to vent?_
- _Frame: what specifically, what impact, what I need_
- _Stay regulated during the conversation; the other person will mirror your state_
- _Listen more than you talk_
- _Close with shared understanding of what happens next_

## 4. In feedback conversations (receiving)

**Protocol:**
- _Notice defensiveness arising in the body_
- _Internal note: "this is hard to hear" — naming reduces grip_
- _Resist explanation / defense in the moment_
- _Genuine curiosity: "tell me more about what you observed"_
- _Sit with the feedback before responding_

## 5. In strategic decisions

**Protocol:**
- _Slow down deliberately — the importance of the decision is the reason to slow, not to rush_
- _Notice what is at stake for you personally — status, identity, narrative_
- _Apply the bias / decision architecture toolkit (see `entscheiden-unter-unsicherheit` skill)_
- _Sit with the decision overnight if possible_
- _Decide from a centered state, not from urgency_

## 6. In team interactions

**Protocol:**
- _Walk into the room with present attention; phone away_
- _Notice the state of others before launching into agenda_
- _Speak less than you used to think you should_
- _When you speak, name what you observe in others where appropriate ("I notice some hesitation...")_
- _Close meetings with what you appreciated, not just what is next_

## 7. In one-on-ones

**Protocol:**
- _2 minutes before: review what is going on for this person beyond the work_
- _Open with genuine question, not status update_
- _80% listening_
- _Ask the deeper question once: "How are you really?"_
- _End with a specific commitment from each side_

## 8. In transitions during the day

**Micro-practices** — 30 seconds to 2 minutes between meetings, contexts, decisions:

- 3 physiological sighs
- Brief body scan (where am I tense)
- Naming: "what state am I in right now?"
- Walking 50 steps with attention on feet, not on next thing
- Drinking water with full attention

## 9. End of day

**Protocol:**
- _Brief review: what served, what didn't, what shifted_
- _Acknowledge what was hard_
- _Note any unfinished emotional business_
- _Transition out of work — clothes, walk, music, ritual_
- _Sleep preparation: low light, no screens, breathing practice if needed_

## 10. When you notice you have lost the practice

_It happens. Travel, crisis, family situation, drift. The practice is not the practice; the return is._

**My return protocol:**
- _Today, even 5 minutes_
- _Tomorrow, same time, same place_
- _Skip the self-criticism — it does no work_
- _Within a week, full practice back_

## 11. What others will notice

_Over weeks of sustained practice, others will notice. Not because you announce it — because your behavior shifts._

- _More present in conversations_
- _Less reactive under pressure_
- _More observant of others' states_
- _Slower to decide; better decisions_
- _Calmer in difficulty_
- _Available without being depleted_

This is what leadership looks like that emerges from mindful self-leadership. The visibility is the proof.
