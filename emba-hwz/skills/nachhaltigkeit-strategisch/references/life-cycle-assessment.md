# Life Cycle Assessment — When and How

LCA is the systematic accounting of environmental impacts of a product or service across its life cycle. ISO 14040 / 14044 standardize the methodology. For executives, the value of LCA is decision-relevant insight, not academic comprehensiveness.

## When to use LCA

- Choosing between product design options
- Identifying the dominant impact stage (often counter-intuitive)
- Substantiating sustainability claims (avoiding greenwashing risk)
- Supporting Scope 3 emissions accounting (especially category 11, use of sold products)
- Designing for circularity (the LCA tells you where impact concentrates)

## The four phases (ISO 14040)

### 1. Goal and scope definition
Define the question, the functional unit (the basis for comparison), the system boundary, the impact categories.

- **Functional unit:** e.g., "1 t-shirt worn 50 times" — not "1 t-shirt." The unit must reflect actual use.
- **System boundary:** cradle-to-gate, cradle-to-grave, cradle-to-cradle. Choose deliberately; document.
- **Impact categories:** at minimum carbon (CO₂e). Often also water, land use, eutrophication, acidification, etc.

### 2. Life cycle inventory
Collect data on inputs and outputs at each life cycle stage: raw materials, manufacturing, distribution, use, end-of-life.

The hard part: data quality varies wildly. Primary data (measured) is gold; database data (Ecoinvent, GaBi) is bronze; estimated data is to be acknowledged as such.

### 3. Impact assessment
Translate the inventory into impact category indicators (kg CO₂e, m³ water-eq, etc.) using characterization factors.

### 4. Interpretation
Identify hotspots, perform sensitivity analysis, draw conclusions, communicate.

## Common findings that surprise executives

- **For laundered apparel:** use phase (washing/drying) is often the dominant impact.
- **For laptops and phones:** manufacturing dominates over use phase (especially with low-carbon electricity).
- **For dairy products:** the farm stage dominates; processing is minor.
- **For shoes:** the leather (or alternative material) often dominates.
- **For furniture:** sometimes packaging and transport are surprisingly high.

The hotspots tell you where redesign matters. Without an LCA, you optimize the wrong thing.

## Streamlined vs. full LCA

- **Full LCA** — ISO-compliant, third-party reviewed, multi-impact-category, multi-month effort. Required for product environmental declarations (EPDs), for some regulatory contexts, for high-stakes claims.
- **Screening LCA** — uses simplified data, focuses on dominant impacts, takes weeks not months. Sufficient for strategic decisions and product design hotspot identification.

For strategy work, screening LCA is usually the right scale. Push to full LCA only when claims need to be substantiated for regulators or external scrutiny.

## LCA software and databases

- **Software:** SimaPro, GaBi (Sphera), OpenLCA (open source)
- **Databases:** Ecoinvent (most comprehensive), GaBi, US LCI
- **Carbon-only simpler tools:** Carbon Trust footprint calculator, various sectoral tools

For a one-off strategic LCA, consultants are often the right answer. For ongoing product design, building internal capability is worth it.

## Common LCA failures

- **Wrong functional unit.** Comparing 1 kg of one product to 1 kg of another when the value delivered is different.
- **Boundary games.** Excluding a stage (e.g., use phase) where the impact concentrates, making the chosen design look better.
- **Cherry-picked impact category.** Showing low carbon while ignoring high water or land use.
- **Stale data.** Using emissions factors from a decade ago for a fast-decarbonizing electricity grid.
- **Comparing dissimilar systems.** A reusable cup vs. a disposable cup needs a comparable use cycle assumption.

A credible LCA passes critical review by people who do not have the same incentives as the team that commissioned it.

## LCA and product claims

Increasingly, regulators are scrutinizing sustainability claims. EU Green Claims Directive (proposed) would require substantiation. CMA, FTC, ASA enforce against unsubstantiated claims. ESRS E1 disclosure requires methodology transparency.

The implication: a sustainability claim should be backed by a defensible LCA. Without that, the claim is exposed.

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 5 of the SKILL.md workflow:

1. Identify the 1–3 products or services where an LCA would change strategy.
2. Scope a screening LCA for each — typically a 4–6 week effort.
3. Use the hotspot findings to guide redesign and target-setting.
4. Keep the LCA defensible enough that any claim derived from it can be substantiated.

An LCA whose findings are surprising is the one that earns its keep. An LCA that confirms what management already believed is often built to confirm.
