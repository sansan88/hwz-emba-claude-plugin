# Materiality, SDGs, and Picking What Matters

The single most useful and least respected discipline in sustainability strategy is *picking what matters*. Most company sustainability reports list 12+ topics with shallow treatment; a sharp materiality assessment narrows to 5–8 topics with the depth that produces strategy.

## Double materiality (CSRD)

The European framework requires consideration of both directions:

- **Impact materiality** — significant impacts (positive or negative) of the company on people, environment, and society
- **Financial materiality** — sustainability-related risks and opportunities that significantly affect the company's prospects

A topic is material under CSRD if it is material from *either* perspective. The two perspectives often overlap; sometimes they diverge.

## How to run a materiality assessment

### Step 1 — Long-list of potential topics
Source from:
- Sector-specific standards (SASB / IFRS S2 industry standards) — what topics are typically material for this sector
- ESRS topical standards — the EU's list
- Peer companies' material topic disclosures
- Stakeholder interviews — what customers, investors, employees, regulators raise
- Internal voices — what the company already knows about its impacts

Expect a long list of 30–50 topics.

### Step 2 — Score each topic on the two axes

For each:
- **Impact significance** — what is the magnitude of the company's impact on this topic? Use the value chain lens. Use available LCA / footprint data.
- **Financial significance** — what are the risks and opportunities? Transition risk, physical risk, regulatory risk, reputation, demand shifts, capital cost.

Score 1–5 on each. Most useful: do it in a workshop with diverse senior input.

### Step 3 — Plot on a 2x2 matrix

Topics in the upper-right (high on both axes) are clearly material. Upper-left or lower-right (high on one) often are material under CSRD double-materiality. Lower-left are not material — set aside.

### Step 4 — Validate

- **External stakeholder validation** — present back to representative stakeholders and ask: did we miss anything, did we overweight anything?
- **Sector benchmark** — does our material topic list make sense compared to credible peers?
- **Honesty test** — is there a topic we are reluctant to include because it is uncomfortable? That is usually a sign it should be included.

### Step 5 — Document

Produce a materiality matrix and topic narrative for each material topic. This becomes the foundation for the strategy and the disclosure.

## The SDG layer

The Sustainable Development Goals are a useful complementary lens but a poor primary strategy tool. The right use:

- **Map the material topics to the SDGs they connect to.** Some material topics map to multiple SDGs.
- **Identify the SDGs where the company has the largest material impact (positive or negative).** Usually 3–5.
- **Don't claim to "contribute to all 17 SDGs."** That is performative and damages credibility.

For Swiss companies, also pay attention to spillover effects — see the `politische-rechtliche-landkarte` skill for the systems lens on SDGs.

## The 5–8 topic discipline

A working materiality outcome:

- 5–8 topics where the company has both material impact and material financial exposure
- For each, a clear statement of how the company is positioned: harm to reduce, opportunity to capture, risk to manage
- Targets and disclosure aligned to these topics
- The "long tail" of 20+ less material topics tracked but not deeply addressed

Companies that try to address 15 topics deeply usually address none deeply. Companies that address 3 topics deeply often miss material exposures.

## Topics commonly under-included

Topics that are often material but under-addressed:

- **Scope 3 emissions categories** beyond the headline number — purchased goods specifics, use phase
- **Water risk** in water-stressed geographies
- **Biodiversity and nature** — emerging under TNFD
- **Human rights in supply chain** — particularly in textiles, electronics, agriculture
- **Just transition** — workforce implications of decarbonization, especially in fossil-dependent regions or roles
- **Lobbying alignment** — whether company lobbying aligns with stated sustainability ambitions
- **Tax behavior** — corporate tax strategy as a sustainability topic in the SDG context

## Topics commonly over-included

- **Vague "innovation"** that does not tie to specific sustainability outcomes
- **Generic "community engagement"** without measurable contribution
- **Photogenic but immaterial topics** picked for the sustainability report visual

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 2 of the SKILL.md workflow:

1. Run the materiality assessment if not already done
2. Push the user to a sharp 5–8 topic list with rationale
3. Stress-test the list against the under-included and over-included patterns
4. Lock the list as the basis for the rest of the strategy

A strategy without a sharp materiality basis becomes a sustainability buffet — comprehensive, unfocused, ineffective.
