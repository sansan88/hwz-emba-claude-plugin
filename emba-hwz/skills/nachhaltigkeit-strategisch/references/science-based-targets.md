# Science-Based Targets — The Operating Standard

The SBTi (Science-Based Targets initiative) is now the de facto global standard for credible corporate climate targets. CSRD and IFRS S2 disclosure increasingly rely on SBTi-aligned language. Even companies that do not formally validate with SBTi typically follow the methodology.

## What SBTi requires

A target is "science-based" if it is in line with what the latest climate science deems necessary to limit warming to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels.

Practically:
- **Scope 1+2 targets** — required for all SBTi-validated companies
- **Scope 3 targets** — required if Scope 3 is more than 40% of total emissions (it usually is)
- **Near-term targets** — 5–10 years out, with at least 4.2% annual reduction (1.5°C aligned)
- **Net-zero targets** — typically 2050, with at least 90% absolute reduction and the remaining 10% addressed through high-quality removals

Specific sector pathways exist for hard-to-abate sectors (cement, steel, aviation, etc.).

## Why this matters strategically

A few reasons science-based targets have become non-negotiable for serious sustainability strategy:

1. **Stakeholder expectation** — institutional investors, B2B procurement, talent.
2. **Regulatory alignment** — CSRD requires disclosure of climate transition plans aligned with 1.5°C; SBTi is the working standard.
3. **Avoids greenwashing risk** — non-SBTi-validated targets are increasingly questioned. Misleading climate claims have triggered enforcement (UK CMA, ASA; US FTC) and litigation.
4. **Operational discipline** — the methodology forces realistic baseline, real Scope 3 accounting, real annual reduction commitments.

## The four-step process

### 1. Commit
Sign the commitment letter to set SBTi targets within 24 months.

### 2. Develop
Build the inventory (Scope 1, 2, 3), set the targets using SBTi methodology, develop the transition plan.

Common methods:
- **Absolute Contraction Approach** — apply the 1.5°C-aligned linear annual reduction (currently 4.2% per year for near-term) to absolute emissions.
- **Sectoral Decarbonization Approach** — sector-specific pathways for heavy-emitting sectors.
- **Physical Intensity Approach** — for some sectors, intensity targets aligned with sector pathway.

### 3. Submit
Submit targets to SBTi for validation. Most companies receive feedback and iterate. Validation takes 3–6 months typically.

### 4. Communicate and disclose
Publish validated targets. Disclose annual progress.

## Common strategy pitfalls

- **Setting targets before having a credible baseline.** Scope 3 is often only roughly estimated when targets are set. Refinement later changes the picture. Better: invest in baseline first.
- **Setting an intensity target without an absolute target.** Intensity targets can coexist with rising absolute emissions if volume grows. SBTi requires absolute reduction at least for Scope 1+2.
- **Net zero by 2050 without a credible near-term pathway.** Stakeholders increasingly scrutinize the 2030 milestone, not the 2050 announcement.
- **No transition plan.** A target without a plan is a wish. CSRD requires a transition plan; SBTi recommends it.
- **Heavy reliance on offsets.** Limited role; high quality only; residual emissions only.

## Sectoral specifics

Some sectors have specific SBTi pathways:

- **Power** — sector-specific carbon intensity pathway
- **Cement** — physical intensity pathway, also CCS dependencies
- **Steel** — physical intensity, hydrogen / DRI pathway
- **Aviation** — well-to-wake emissions, sustainable aviation fuels
- **Shipping** — well-to-wake, alternative fuels
- **Oil & gas** — specific guidance acknowledging value-chain emissions dominance
- **Real estate** — pathway for building operations

For financial institutions, the SBTi *Financial Sector Net-Zero Standard* covers financed and facilitated emissions.

## Linking targets to strategy

The targets are not the strategy — they are constraints on the strategy. Useful questions to test the connection:

- **What product changes are required to hit the target?** Specific products, not abstract "innovation."
- **What supply chain changes?** Which suppliers, on what timeline?
- **What capex is required?** Which investments, when, at what scale?
- **What revenue trade-offs?** Will we shed high-emission product lines? Customer segments?
- **What capability builds?** Energy, materials, design, supplier engagement.

If the targets do not show up in capex plans, R&D priorities, supplier contracts, and product decisions, the targets are aspirations.

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 4 of the SKILL.md workflow, help the user:

1. Decide whether to formally validate with SBTi or use the methodology privately
2. Identify the baseline gaps that block target-setting
3. Test the proposed targets against the strategy and capex plans
4. Surface the trade-offs explicit in the target

A credible target is one the user could defend to a skeptical board member with the underlying transition plan and trade-offs visible.
