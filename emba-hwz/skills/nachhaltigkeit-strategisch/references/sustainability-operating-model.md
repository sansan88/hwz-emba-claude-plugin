# Sustainability Operating Model — Embedding Through Structure

The 6P framework summary, plus the operating-model patterns from Bain and McKinsey research on what actually works.

## The 6P framework

Six dimensions where sustainability has to show up if it is going to stick:

### 1. Purpose
Why sustainability matters for *this specific company* — not in general. The connection to value creation, customer relevance, employee meaning, license to operate. If the purpose is performative, everything below collapses.

### 2. People
Who is accountable. The CEO and board, in the first instance — sustainability that is not on the CEO's agenda does not happen. Then the named sustainability function, the integration into business unit leadership, the talent pipeline, the capability building.

### 3. Processes
How sustainability is embedded in the operating processes of the company:
- Procurement (supplier requirements, audits)
- R&D and product development (eco-design, LCA gates)
- Capital allocation (carbon prices, sustainability hurdles)
- M&A (sustainability due diligence)
- Risk management (transition risk, physical risk)
- Reporting and disclosure

### 4. Practices
The daily behaviors and rituals. The way meetings include sustainability dimensions. The way decisions surface trade-offs. The way the language of the company refers to sustainability. Culture in operating detail.

### 5. Products
What the company sells. The most consequential dimension. Sustainability strategy that does not eventually change the product portfolio is bounded by intensity reduction alone.

### 6. Performance
How sustainability is measured, reported, and tied to incentives:
- Internal scorecards
- External disclosure (CSRD, IFRS S2, GRI, CDP)
- Executive compensation linkage
- Capital costs and sustainability-linked finance

A balanced sustainability operating model invests in all six. Bias toward "we have a sustainability strategy and disclose it" is the most common form of incompleteness.

## The four operating model patterns (Bain, McKinsey synthesis)

### Pattern A — Centralized
Strong central sustainability function leads. Business units consume policy, support reporting.

**Pros:** Strong standards, single voice, faster initial deployment.
**Cons:** Risk of being seen as cost/compliance center; business units don't own sustainability.
**Best when:** Early in the journey, low maturity, regulated industries, need for fast standardization.

### Pattern B — Embedded
Sustainability owned by business units. Small central team for standards and reporting.

**Pros:** High business ownership, faster execution at the BU level.
**Cons:** Standards drift, fragmented reporting, weaker overall narrative.
**Best when:** High maturity, strong general management, the strategy is largely about operational reduction.

### Pattern C — Hybrid (most common)
Central team owns: standards, reporting, methodology, supplier program, climate strategy. Business units own: implementation, product changes, customer engagement.

**Pros:** Balances depth and breadth. Standards plus ownership.
**Cons:** Requires deliberate design of decision rights and handoffs.
**Best when:** Mid-to-high maturity, multi-business-unit, mid-sized companies. Now the most common pattern.

### Pattern D — Integrated leadership
CEO and CFO co-own sustainability as a core business agenda. Small specialized function for expertise. Sustainability is part of strategy, not adjacent to it.

**Pros:** Highest legitimacy, fastest cycle time, deep integration into capital allocation.
**Cons:** Requires senior leadership capability and conviction. Rare.
**Best when:** Sustainability is genuinely strategic to the company's value creation, and leadership is genuinely committed.

## The role of the Chief Sustainability Officer (CSO)

A useful test of seriousness: where does the CSO sit in the organization?

- **Reporting to comms or government affairs:** sustainability is positioning, not operations
- **Reporting to operations or supply chain:** sustainability is being treated as operational reduction
- **Reporting to strategy or directly to CEO:** sustainability is strategic
- **CFO as CSO (some leading companies):** sustainability is integrated into capital allocation

The reporting line is the strategy signal.

## What makes the operating model work

A few patterns from companies that have made the transition:

1. **Senior leadership has personally engaged.** Not delegated. CEO and board members can speak to sustainability strategy in detail without the CSO in the room.
2. **Sustainability metrics in executive compensation.** Typically 10–25% of long-term incentive, tied to credible targets. Without this, sustainability competes against quarterly results and loses.
3. **Procurement is wired in.** Supplier sustainability requirements are real, audited, and consequential. Not a survey.
4. **Capital allocation is wired in.** Internal carbon price, sustainability hurdle rates, dedicated sustainability investment envelope.
5. **The CFO is fluent.** CSRD and IFRS S2 disclosure pulls the CFO in; that pull is the integration mechanism.
6. **R&D gates include LCA / impact.** New product development cannot proceed past a stage gate without sustainability assessment.

These are operational details. They are also where strategy becomes real.

## Common failures

- **Bolted-on CSO.** Senior title, no team, no budget, no decision rights. Predictably fails.
- **Sustainability silo.** Function reports out separately, business units treat it as not theirs.
- **No CFO engagement.** Without finance leadership, sustainability stays soft.
- **Compensation talk, no compensation action.** Sustainability metrics announced but not weighted meaningfully.
- **No data infrastructure.** ESG metrics depend on data systems that don't exist; reporting is heroic effort each cycle.

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 7 of the SKILL.md workflow:

1. Assess where the user's organization is today on the 6P framework
2. Identify which pattern (A, B, C, D) currently describes the operating model
3. Identify the binding constraint — which P or operating model feature is most limiting
4. Propose the 12-month moves to address that constraint
5. Be specific about reporting lines, decision rights, and compensation
