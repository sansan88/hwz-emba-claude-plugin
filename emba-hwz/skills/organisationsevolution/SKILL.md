# Transformative Organisationsevolution — Iterative Build-Measure-Learn for the Whole Organization

This skill operationalizes EMBA Block 4 Tag 3 (Markus Stoisser, HWZ). The premise: developing future-fitness in an organization requires the *iterative co-design* of three interlocking layers — strategy, organizational systems, and culture — across three levels of change — individual, collective, organizational.

Teams are the central lever. Frameworks alone do not change organizations; teams that *experience* new ways of working, in playful and structured ways, do. The methods drawn on include behavioral economics, Design Thinking, Lean Startup's Build-Measure-Learn, agile organizational development (Östereich/Schröder), and Management 3.0 (Appelo).

This skill complements but is distinct from `psychologie-veraenderung` (which focuses on the psychology of unlearning) and `regenerative-organisation` (which focuses on the *what* of "good" organizing). This skill focuses on the *how* — the practical methods and operating system for evolving the organization through team-based iteration.

## When to lean on this skill

Use it when the user is: designing an agile or modern way of working, evolving organizational structure, building high-performing teams, applying playful facilitation methods (LEGO SP, Eigenland, Metro-Mapping), aligning strategy/structure/culture, running team-based workshops with real outcomes, or operationalizing Build-Measure-Learn at the org level.

Do not use it for: pure technical / IT transformation (different toolkit), individual coaching (different focus), or short-cycle product Build-Measure-Learn (different scale).

## Mental model: the three layers, three levels, three loops

A useful integrating frame:

| | Strategy | Systems | Culture |
|---|----------|---------|---------|
| **Individual** | What I am for | What enables/constrains me | What I value and how I work |
| **Collective (team)** | Team purpose | Team practices | Team norms |
| **Organizational** | Org direction | Org structure | Org culture |

Evolution requires moving forward iteratively in *all nine cells*. Most transformation initiatives privilege one or two — usually org-level systems — and underwork the others. The result is the predictable pattern of "transformation that didn't change anything."

The Build-Measure-Learn loop applies to each cell: design a small experiment, measure what happens, learn, iterate. The skill is making this concrete at organizational scale.

## Workflow

### 1. Locate the work across the matrix

Use `templates/three-layers-three-levels-canvas.md`. For the user's evolution, map current state and target state across all nine cells. Identify which cells are currently being worked on, which are underworked, and where the binding constraint sits.

### 2. Identify the focal teams

Teams are the lever. Pick 2–4 teams that will be the focal sites of the evolution work. Criteria:

- They span the work the organization actually does (not all in one function)
- They include senior representatives — leaders not just project teams
- They have enough authority to actually change things they decide to change
- They have appetite — willingness to do the work

Read `references/high-performing-teams.md` for what makes a team capable of being the lever.

### 3. Apply behavioral economics

Read `references/applied-behavioral-economics.md`. Behavioral economics provides the practical bridge between knowing what should change and getting people to actually engage with the change. Specific applications:

- **Defaults** — what is the default behavior, what would it cost to make the new pattern the default
- **Anchoring** — what numbers / examples are anchoring current expectations
- **Loss framing** — what people fear losing in the change (often more motivating than potential gain)
- **Social proof** — which peers are already doing what we want others to do
- **Effort costs** — what friction is in the way; can we reduce it
- **Identity** — who do people think they are; the change has to be consistent with or expand that identity

These principles inform every experiment design and every facilitation choice.

### 4. Choose the methods toolkit

Read `references/playful-methods.md`. The module-specific methods:

- **LEGO® SERIOUS PLAY®** — for Zielbild (target picture) development, complex system mapping, surfacing what is hard to verbalize directly
- **Eigenland®** — for assessment and group dialogue, particularly for current-state diagnosis
- **Metro-Mapping** — for visualizing organizational journeys, interconnections, and decision paths
- **Management 3.0 tools** — Moving Motivators, Kudo Cards, Delegation Poker, Personal Maps, etc.
- **Design Thinking** — for human-centered framing
- **Real Time Strategy for Teams & Enterprises** — for collaborative strategic alignment

These are not gimmicks. Used well, they produce conversations and insights that traditional discussion formats do not. The play is the work.

### 5. Design the Build-Measure-Learn cycle

Use `templates/build-measure-learn-org.md`. At organizational scale, the cycle:

- **Build:** design and implement a small organizational intervention — a new team practice, a structural tweak, a leadership behavior, a tool, a decision rule
- **Measure:** observe what happens — both quantitatively (productivity, cycle time, decisions made) and qualitatively (energy, voice, conflict patterns)
- **Learn:** reflect together; what does this tell us; what was surprising; what shifts our model of how this organization works
- **Iterate:** refine, scale, or pivot

Cadence: 4–8 week cycles for most organizational experiments.

### 6. Build the high-performing team layer

Read `references/high-performing-teams.md`. The Google Aristotle research (and Hackman's earlier work) converged on consistent findings about what makes teams high-performing:

1. **Psychological safety** (see `psychologie-veraenderung` skill)
2. **Dependability** — team members reliably do what they say
3. **Structure and clarity** — clear roles, plans, goals
4. **Meaning** — work has personal significance
5. **Impact** — work matters and changes things

For each focal team in the evolution work, diagnose against these factors. Where weak, the team cannot serve as a transformation lever — fix first.

### 7. Connect to strategy through Real Time Strategy

The Real Time Strategy for Teams & Enterprises method (Robert "Jake" Jacobs) brings 50–500+ people into a room to co-develop strategic direction. Adapted from the original Whole-Scale Change method.

Useful for the moments in organizational evolution where strategy and broad ownership need to be developed together — typically once or twice in a transformation arc, not continuously.

### 8. Compose the evolution roadmap

The roadmap is not a Gantt chart. It is a sequence of experiments across the matrix, anchored by the focal teams, supported by playful methods, run on Build-Measure-Learn cycles. Use `templates/evolution-roadmap.md`.

## Output style

Energetic, practical, ready to do the work. Acknowledge that this is craft as well as theory. Recommend specific methods for specific moments rather than generic frameworks. Be willing to design the actual workshop, not just talk about workshops.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- **Method tourism.** Trying every playful tool once, sticking with none. The tools earn value through repeated use.
- **Team-as-buzzword.** Using "team" generically without diagnosing whether the actual unit functions as a team. Many "teams" are working groups.
- **Single-layer focus.** Evolving systems without culture; or culture without strategy. The triangle is interconnected.
- **One-off workshop dependency.** A LEGO SP session that does not lead to operating changes is corporate entertainment.
- **Behavioral economics as manipulation.** The tools work for change, not against people. Used manipulatively, they corrode trust.
- **Endless iteration without commitment.** Build-Measure-Learn applied as permanent agility-theater, never converging on operating changes that stick.
