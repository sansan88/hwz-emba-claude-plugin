# Playful Methods for Organizational Evolution

The methods covered in this module — LEGO® SERIOUS PLAY®, Eigenland®, Metro-Mapping, Management 3.0 tools — share a family resemblance: they use embodied, visual, or play-based forms to surface what verbal discussion alone cannot.

The thesis: play is not the opposite of serious work; play is *the form* serious work often takes when it requires surfacing what is implicit, complex, or emotional. Used well, these methods produce conversations that would not happen in a meeting room.

## LEGO® SERIOUS PLAY® (LSP)

A facilitated method using LEGO bricks to build metaphors and explore complex topics. Originating from MIT and LEGO research in the late 1990s.

### How it works
Participants build LEGO models in response to specific prompts. The models represent ideas, situations, or visions. The act of building thinks differently from verbal articulation — the hands lead the mind into ideas that would not have surfaced otherwise. After building, each participant shares the meaning of their model.

### When to use
- Developing a shared Zielbild (target picture) — see also `strategie-als-kompass/references/zukunftsbild.md`
- Surfacing complex system dynamics
- Building shared understanding across diverse views
- When verbal discussion is stalling because the topic is hard to articulate directly

### When not to use
- Simple, well-bounded discussions that don't need depth
- Highly task-focused execution meetings
- Groups that will be uncomfortable enough with the method that the discomfort overwhelms the benefit

### Sequence for a Zielbild session
1. Warm-up build (low stakes, gets people building)
2. Individual build of "where we are today"
3. Sharing and listening
4. Individual build of "where we want to be in 2030" (or relevant horizon)
5. Sharing and listening
6. Group build of integrated future
7. Group build of "what gets in the way"
8. Group build of "first moves"
9. Documentation and commitment

A full session takes 4–6 hours. Shorter versions exist but lose much of the value.

### Requires
A trained facilitator. The method has been certified by LEGO; not facilitated improvisationally.

## Eigenland®

A discussion and assessment method using stylized "islands" or "countries" on a board. Participants position their views, vote on statements, and explore disagreement.

### How it works
The facilitator presents statements or questions; participants vote with tokens on a stylized landscape. The visual representation of where the group stands — and where it diverges — produces conversation that scattered comments would not.

### When to use
- Current-state assessments
- Identifying gaps between perception and behavior
- Surfacing disagreement in a low-conflict way
- Producing data for next-step conversations

### Strengths
Surfaces dissent quickly. Visual. Engaging.

### Limitations
Quality depends on statement design. Poor statements produce noise.

## Metro-Mapping

Visualizing organizational journeys, decision paths, or initiatives as metro/subway map lines. Stations are key decisions or milestones; line crossings are interconnections.

### When to use
- Showing the connections between multiple initiatives
- Mapping the decision journey of a customer or employee
- Identifying where different streams intersect
- Producing a shared visual artifact that lives beyond the workshop

### Strengths
Strong as documentation. Survives the workshop. Easy to update.

### Limitations
Can become decorative without rigor in the underlying analysis.

## Management 3.0 toolkit (Appelo)

A set of practical tools for team leadership and motivation. Each tool is small, repeatable, and addresses a specific aspect of team dynamics.

### Selected tools

**Moving Motivators** — Each person ranks the 10 intrinsic motivators (acceptance, curiosity, freedom, status, goal, etc.) for themselves. Surfaces what motivates each team member; informs how to design work and team norms.

**Personal Maps** — Each person creates a mind-map of themselves (family, interests, history, etc.). Shared in the team. Builds human connection and reduces faceless-coworker dynamics.

**Kudo Cards** — Structured peer recognition. Concrete, specific, public. Builds the recognition habit; reinforces desired behaviors.

**Delegation Poker** — A team uses cards to surface implicit assumptions about decision rights for various decisions. Reveals where the team thinks delegation should be (and where it currently is).

**Celebration Grid** — A grid distinguishing learning from outcome quality. Celebrates good experiments regardless of outcome; learns from poor practices that happened to work.

**Yay! Questions** — A set of prompts for retrospectives that surface what is working, what could be better, what to change.

### When to use
Continuously, in small doses, baked into team rhythms. Not as workshop-once-and-forget. The value compounds.

## Design Thinking

A human-centered approach to problem framing and solution development. Phases: Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test.

### When to use
- New product or service design
- Process redesign with human users
- Re-framing problems when the team is stuck
- Building user empathy in technical teams

### Common in evolution work for
- Customer or employee experience redesign
- Re-framing a transformation problem from the affected user's perspective
- Generating divergent options before converging

## Lean Startup Build-Measure-Learn

Originally for startups; widely applied to organizational change.

### Cycle
Build a minimum viable version → Measure what happens → Learn from the data → Refine, scale, or pivot.

### In organizational work
The "product" is an intervention or new way of working. The MVP is the smallest experiment that meaningfully tests the hypothesis. The learning is iterative.

See also the `psychologie-veraenderung` skill for deeper treatment of experiments.

## Real Time Strategy for Teams & Enterprises (Jacobs)

A large-group intervention bringing 50–500+ people together for collaborative strategy development. Compresses what would otherwise be months of cascade into a few intense days.

### When to use
At inflection points in evolution work where broad ownership of the next direction is essential. Typically 1–2 times in a multi-year transformation arc.

### Requires
Significant logistics and skilled facilitation. Not a casual workshop.

## Choosing the right method

Match method to moment:

| Moment | Method |
|--------|--------|
| Developing target picture | LEGO® SERIOUS PLAY® |
| Current-state assessment | Eigenland®, Edmondson safety scale |
| Visualizing complexity | Metro-Mapping |
| Building team dynamics | Management 3.0 tools |
| Reframing problems | Design Thinking |
| Testing interventions | Build-Measure-Learn |
| Large-group alignment | Real Time Strategy |
| Reflective conversation | Retrospective formats |

Not every moment needs a specialized method. Pure structured conversation often serves. Use playful methods when they earn their keep.

## Method failures

- **Method as theater.** Using playful methods to make boring topics feel exciting, without commitment to the resulting outputs.
- **Tourist mode.** Trying every method once, integrating none into the operating rhythm.
- **Unprepared facilitation.** LSP, Eigenland, etc. require real facilitation skill. Botched facilitation produces cynicism.
- **Over-fitting to method.** Forcing the situation into the method rather than choosing the method that fits.
- **One-off culture project.** A playful workshop that does not lead to changes in the daily operating rhythm.

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 4 of the SKILL.md workflow, choose 2–3 methods that fit the evolution work. Schedule them at the right moments in the arc. Plan the facilitation deliberately. Insist that workshop outputs translate into changes in the operating rhythm, not just into PowerPoint summaries.
