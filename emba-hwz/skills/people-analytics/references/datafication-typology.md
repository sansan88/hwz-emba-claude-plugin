# The Bad, the Good, and the Greater Good

Schafheitle & Weibel's typology of datafication control architectures. The single most useful diagnostic for People Analytics programs.

## Why a typology

People Analytics programs all look similar on the surface — dashboards, scores, predictions. The differences live in *what gets measured, by whom, used how, with what voice*. Those differences produce dramatically different outcomes for trust, performance, retention, and legitimacy.

The typology gives leaders a vocabulary to locate their program honestly and to design changes.

## Type 1 — The "bad": datafication as surveillance

**Core logic:** Maximize management visibility into worker behavior. Asymmetric — workers are visible, management is not. Used for performance management, ranking, micro-correction.

**Examples:**
- Productivity scoring based on keystroke logs, mouse activity, screen time
- Activity monitoring software that captures application usage in real time
- "Engagement" scores derived from collaboration patterns, used in individual performance reviews
- Real-time location tracking beyond legitimate operational need
- AI predictions about individual flight risk, used to pre-empt promotions or restructure roles

**Effects observed in research and practice:**
- Workers learn to game the metrics (meeting attendance for the sake of attendance, performative typing)
- The best workers leave first — they have options and refuse to work under surveillance
- Innovation collapses — explorative work that doesn't show up in metrics is disincentivized
- Legal and reputational risk accumulates
- Apparent productivity rises briefly, real productivity declines

**Where it shows up:** Often in companies with a strong control culture, in industries with thin margins, or in remote-work panics where management feels visibility loss.

## Type 2 — The "good": datafication as evidence

**Core logic:** Replace gut-feel decisions with data-informed decisions. Less asymmetric — data is used for organizational decisions, not for individual punishment. Aggregated rather than individualized.

**Examples:**
- Engagement surveys analyzed at team/department level for organizational improvement
- Workforce composition analytics for diversity programs
- Skills inventory analytics for strategic workforce planning
- Aggregated collaboration network analysis to identify silos
- Attrition analysis at population level to inform retention investment

**Effects observed:**
- Better organizational decisions on average than gut alone
- Management more accountable to data, but the data still represents what management chose to measure
- Limited workforce trust impact — neutral to mildly positive
- Risk of false precision (mistaking measurement for understanding)
- Risk of slippage toward Type 1 over time as the same data becomes available individually

**Where it shows up:** Most "mature" corporate People Analytics functions today land here. It is a real improvement over gut decisions. It is also where many programs plateau.

## Type 3 — The "greater good": datafication as shared understanding

**Core logic:** Data is collected with worker voice in scope and use. Used symmetrically — leadership behavior is measured by the same instruments. Insights are shared back to the workforce, not just used by management. The aim is shared understanding, not management visibility.

**Examples:**
- Manager-quality metrics (response time, span-of-control health, retention of direct reports) reported with the same granularity as IC output
- Engagement data shared back to teams with the team's own choice on what to act on
- Collaboration network analysis where teams use the data on themselves to design their own ways of working
- Algorithmic decision audits where bias findings are made transparent to affected workers
- Joint employee-management governance of which metrics are added or retired

**Effects observed:**
- Higher trust, better engagement, better retention of senior talent
- Slower decisions on the surface, better decisions over the medium term
- Requires a management culture that can accept being measured itself

**Where it shows up:** Rare in pure form. Closest analogues in companies with strong co-determination (German Mitbestimmung, Swiss Sozialpartnerschaft, employee-owned firms).

## Where most real programs sit

Most People Analytics programs are mixed. They contain Type 2 components and creeping Type 1 components, usually because vendors add features and managers gradually start using them for individual decisions. The drift is rarely deliberate.

The diagnostic move: walk through the program's active use cases, classify each, and surface the drift. Programs labeled as Type 2 internally often have Type 1 use cases inside them.

## Designing toward Type 3 — practical levers

If the user wants to shift the program toward "greater good":

1. **Add symmetric measurement.** Pick three metrics about leadership behavior that go alongside the worker metrics. Report them at the same level of granularity.
2. **Give workforce voice in scope.** A real (not advisory) decision body that includes worker representation, with authority to veto specific use cases.
3. **Share back what is found.** Insights from the data should reach the workforce, not just management.
4. **Build in sunset clauses.** Every use case has a default expiration date. Renewal requires evidence of continued value and acceptable cost.
5. **Audit by someone independent.** Internal audit, not the analytics team itself.

These are not nice-to-haves. They are what makes the program survivable in a world of increasing scrutiny.

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 1 of the SKILL.md workflow, walk through each significant use case in the program and classify it. State to the user:

- The most "bad" use case in your program is X. Here is why it sits there and what would move it.
- The most "greater good" use case in your program is Y. Here is what makes it work.
- The overall portfolio leans toward [type] because [pattern].

Naming the pattern is itself the unlock — most executives have never explicitly classified.
