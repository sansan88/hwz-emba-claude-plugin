# Council of Europe, EU & the European Rights Architecture

The biggest source of confusion for executives is conflating the Council of Europe (CoE) with the European Union (EU). They are separate organizations with different mandates, but both create binding rules that touch corporate practice. This reference makes the distinction operational.

## The two systems at a glance

| Dimension | Council of Europe | European Union |
|-----------|------------------|----------------|
| Founded | 1949 | 1958 (EEC) / 1993 (EU) |
| Members | 46 states (after Russia's expulsion 2022) | 27 member states |
| Core instrument | European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR, 1950) | Treaties (TEU, TFEU), Charter of Fundamental Rights |
| Court | European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR), Strasbourg | Court of Justice of the EU (CJEU), Luxembourg |
| Binding on Switzerland | **Yes** (ECHR ratified 1974) | Mostly via bilateral agreements, not direct |
| Domain | Human rights, democracy, rule of law | Single market, customs, competition, growing rights |

For a Swiss company: ECtHR judgments bind Switzerland; EU law touches you when you do business in or with the EU. Both regimes are tightening on rights-related corporate practice.

## ECHR articles that matter for business

- **Art. 6 — Right to a fair trial.** Affects employment law, due process in dismissals.
- **Art. 8 — Right to private and family life.** Now the legal base for climate, data protection, and workplace privacy claims.
- **Art. 10 — Freedom of expression.** Affects employee speech, whistleblower protection, corporate communications.
- **Art. 11 — Freedom of assembly and association.** Affects collective bargaining and union recognition.
- **Art. 14 — Prohibition of discrimination.** Reads onto AI-driven hiring, credit scoring.
- **Protocol 1, Art. 1 — Protection of property.** Affects expropriation, regulatory takings.

## The KlimaSeniorinnen judgment (2024) — what it changes

In April 2024, the Grand Chamber of the ECtHR ruled in *Verein KlimaSeniorinnen Schweiz et al. v. Switzerland* that Switzerland had violated Article 8 ECHR by failing to implement adequate climate policy. Key practical implications:

1. **Climate inaction is a rights violation.** This is the first time a court has explicitly linked inadequate climate policy to a binding human rights treaty.
2. **Standing was granted to an association** (the senior women's group), expanding who can bring climate cases.
3. **State obligations** include setting and meeting concrete GHG reduction targets with adequate intermediate steps.
4. **Spillover to corporate liability** — while the case bound the state, follow-on litigation against high-emitting corporations is now substantially more viable under the rights framing.

For executive practice:
- Assume climate disclosure scrutiny will deepen (not just CSRD, but rights-based litigation risk).
- Treat climate transition plans as legal documents, not marketing.
- For Swiss companies in particular: the state's exposure cascades onto the major emitters.

## The Council of Europe AI Framework Convention (2024)

Opened for signature May 2024. The first international, legally binding instrument on AI specifically focused on human rights, democracy, and the rule of law. It applies to AI activities by **public authorities and private actors performing functions of a public nature, and by private actors more broadly when state parties choose to extend it.**

Key obligations require parties to ensure:
- Human dignity and autonomy are protected
- Equality and non-discrimination in AI design and use
- Privacy and personal data protection
- Transparency and oversight
- Accountability and remedy for harms
- Reliability and safety throughout the lifecycle

For executives:
- This is a *framework* convention — implementation will happen through national law. Watch your operating jurisdictions.
- It overlaps but does not duplicate the EU AI Act; the Council of Europe instrument is broader in geographic scope, more principles-based, and binds states to extend protections.

## The EU AI Act — corporate cheat sheet

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. Risk-based architecture:

- **Unacceptable risk** (prohibited): social scoring, untargeted facial recognition scraping, manipulative AI exploiting vulnerabilities.
- **High risk** (heavy obligations): AI in HR, credit scoring, education, critical infrastructure, biometric ID, law enforcement, migration.
- **Limited risk** (transparency obligations): chatbots, generated content disclosure.
- **Minimal risk** (light touch).

General-purpose AI (GPAI) models have separate, tiered obligations including transparency and, above a compute threshold, systemic risk evaluations.

Timeline (key dates):
- **Feb 2025** — prohibitions and AI literacy obligations apply.
- **Aug 2025** — GPAI obligations apply.
- **Aug 2026** — most other provisions apply.
- **Aug 2027** — high-risk AI in regulated products fully applies.

For executive practice:
- Inventory AI uses against the risk classes now.
- For HR-related AI, assume high-risk classification and prepare documentation, oversight, and bias testing.
- Build the compliance posture as a competitive feature where possible (especially toward conservative B2B buyers).

## Anti-discrimination and AI bias

ECHR Article 14 plus the EU Equal Treatment directives plus the AI Act create overlapping obligations. The Council of Europe's CETS 225 (above) tightens further.

The corporate failure mode: assuming algorithmic bias is a technical problem ("we'll fix the model"). It is an adaptive problem — the data, the proxies, the use case, and the deployment context all carry value judgments that need to be surfaced. See `adaptive-vs-technical.md`.

## CSDDD (Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive)

Directive (EU) 2024/1760, in force July 2024, member state transposition by July 2026.

- Scope: very large companies (initially >5000 employees and €1.5bn turnover, phased down).
- Obligations: identify, prevent, and remedy adverse human rights and environmental impacts in own operations and value chain.
- Climate transition plan obligation aligned with 1.5°C pathway.

For Swiss companies: not directly bound, but bound *de facto* via EU customers and via parallel Swiss developments (Konzernverantwortungs-debate continues).
