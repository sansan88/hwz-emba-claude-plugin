# Purpose Driven Companies — Brand & Strategy Layer of Purpose

This skill operationalizes EMBA Block 6 Tag 1 (Pam Hügli, HWZ). It focuses on purpose as a *strategic management and branding* tool — how purpose shapes customer relationships, brand loyalty, innovation pipeline, employer brand, and risk profile.

This skill complements but is distinct from:

- `regenerative-organisation` skill — which addresses purpose as *organizational design and integrity question*
- `sinnstiftendes-leadership` skill — which addresses *individual meaning and dialogue*

This skill is for the executive who needs to take purpose from concept to brand-and-business outcome. The frameworks here (Sinek's Golden Circle, Aaker's Signature Programs) are deliberately commercial and pragmatic.

## When to lean on this skill

Use it when the user is: defining or rebuilding corporate purpose, brand purpose, mission and vision; designing signature purpose programs; building a purpose-led marketing strategy; aligning employer brand with purpose; defending purpose investment to a skeptical board; or trying to move from a generic purpose statement to one that actually shapes brand and business.

Do not use it when the question is fundamentally about organizational design integrity (use `regenerative-organisation`) or individual meaning (use `sinnstiftendes-leadership`). Purpose work is multilayered; pick the right tool for the layer.

## Mental model: the three concentric purpose questions

Sinek's Golden Circle, adapted and extended:

1. **Why** — why this company exists, what contribution it is committed to. Beyond profit.
2. **How** — the distinctive way it pursues the why. Values, principles, signature practices.
3. **What** — the products and services that embody the how, in service of the why.

Most companies communicate from "what" outward. Purpose-driven companies communicate from "why" outward. The order shapes how customers, employees, and investors relate to the brand.

The Aaker extension: a strong purpose needs **Signature Programs** — sustained, identifiable, owned initiatives that make the purpose visible in the world. Otherwise the why stays abstract.

## Workflow

### 1. Distinguish purpose from mission, vision, values

Read `references/definitions-and-distinctions.md`. These four terms are commonly conflated. The distinctions matter:

- **Purpose (Why)** — the larger contribution. Why we exist. Beyond financial.
- **Mission** — what we do, for whom. The current business focus.
- **Vision** — where we are going. The aspiration.
- **Values** — how we behave. The principles that guide.

A complete framework has all four, with purpose at the top. Most companies have all four written somewhere, with most of them not differentiated meaningfully.

### 2. Develop or refresh the purpose statement

Read `references/developing-purpose.md`. The discipline:

- Inside-out, not marketing-out (see `regenerative-organisation/references/inside-out-purpose.md`)
- Specific to this company, not generic
- Testable — can guide actual decisions
- Stable across the 10–20 year horizon

Use `templates/purpose-statement-canvas.md`. The output is not the words — it is the underlying clarity. The words can iterate; the clarity is the foundation.

### 3. Build the Golden Circle

Read `references/golden-circle-applied.md`. Translate the purpose into Sinek's three-layer model:

- **Why** — the purpose, articulated for stakeholders
- **How** — 3–5 distinctive principles, values, or practices
- **What** — the products, services, programs, ways of operating

The Golden Circle is not just a communication frame — it is a strategic discipline. If the "how" and "what" do not reliably express the "why," the purpose is decorative.

### 4. Design Signature Programs (Aaker)

Read `references/signature-programs.md`. David Aaker's central insight: purpose becomes real through *signature programs* — sustained, identifiable, owned initiatives that visibly embody the purpose.

Examples:
- Dove's "Real Beauty" (decades-long sustained program around redefining beauty)
- Patagonia's environmental activism programs (Worn Wear, Footprint Chronicles)
- Microsoft's accessibility programs (Inclusive Design, sustainability cloud commitments)
- IKEA's circular hubs

For the user, identify 1–3 signature programs that:

- Embody the purpose visibly
- Are sustained over years (not campaigns)
- Are owned by the company (not just sponsored)
- Produce real impact (not just visibility)
- Connect to the business model

Use `templates/signature-program-design.md`.

### 5. Map the purpose impact

Read `references/purpose-impact-areas.md`. Purpose, when authentic and operationalized, shapes:

- **Customer relationships** — preference, loyalty, lifetime value
- **Brand loyalty and pricing power** — premium that purpose-aligned customers will pay
- **Innovation pipeline** — what new products fit the purpose lens
- **Employee attraction and retention** — purpose is a top recruiting attractor
- **Risk management** — purpose-aligned companies have stronger crisis response and stakeholder support

Make these connections explicit. A purpose without measurable impact in at least 2–3 of these areas is not yet operational.

### 6. Test against the purpose-washing risk

Read `references/avoiding-purpose-washing.md`. The single biggest risk: claiming purpose that the company's behavior does not actually support. The cost of getting caught is high; the cost of erosion of internal credibility even when not caught is higher.

Apply the tests:

- **Behavior test** — would a journalist who shadowed the company for a month see the purpose lived?
- **Decision test** — can you point to 3 recent decisions where the purpose meaningfully shaped the choice?
- **Trade-off test** — what does the company decline to do in service of the purpose?
- **Signature test** — what are the named, sustained programs?

If the company fails these tests, the work is not new copy — it is closing the gap between claim and behavior.

### 7. Operationalize the brand and communication

Use `templates/purpose-communication-playbook.md`. Translate the purpose into:

- **Brand narrative** — the story for external audiences
- **Internal narrative** — the story for employees, similar but with different emphasis
- **Touchpoint integration** — where purpose shows up in customer interactions
- **Campaign architecture** — how marketing investment connects to purpose
- **Crisis playbook** — how the purpose guides response to negative events

## Output style

Crisp and pragmatic. Avoid the inspirational-poster register that purpose work often defaults to. The strongest purpose statements sound like the company; the weakest sound like every other company.

Make the business case visible. Purpose is not a CSR side project — done right, it is a strategic moat. Communicate that to a skeptical CFO with specifics.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- **Generic purpose.** "Inspiring people to live better lives" — could be any company. Useless.
- **Purpose as marketing campaign.** A campaign comes and goes. A purpose endures. The two are different tools.
- **Hijacked by external events.** Companies rushing to attach to whatever cause is trending. Inauthentic; quickly seen through.
- **Decoupled from business model.** Purpose that does not connect to what the company actually does. Confused and unpersuasive.
- **No signature programs.** Stated purpose without sustained programs is wallpaper.
- **Misaligned KPIs.** Purpose claimed; metrics rewarded exclusively for short-term financial outcomes. Internal contradiction observed by everyone.
- **Greenwashing / purposewashing.** Increasingly legally risky (EU Green Claims Directive, CMA, FTC) and reputationally fatal.
