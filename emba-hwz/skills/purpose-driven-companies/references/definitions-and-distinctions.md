# Purpose, Mission, Vision, Values — The Distinctions

The four terms are constantly conflated. Untangling them produces clarity and improves every step that follows.

## Purpose

**What it is:** The larger contribution the company makes. The reason for existing beyond producing profit.

**Time horizon:** Stable across 10–20 years and beyond.

**Beneficiary:** The wider world — customers, society, the systems the company touches.

**Tests:**
- Is it about contribution, not just commercial activity?
- Would it remain true if the specific products evolved?
- Does it speak to why anyone (employee, customer, investor) should care beyond price?

**Examples (illustrative; check whether actual behavior supports them):**
- Patagonia: "We're in business to save our home planet."
- Tesla: "Accelerate the world's transition to sustainable energy."
- IKEA: "To create a better everyday life for the many people."

## Mission

**What it is:** What the company does and for whom — now. The current business focus.

**Time horizon:** 3–5 years; can evolve as strategy evolves.

**Beneficiary:** Specific customer segments.

**Tests:**
- Is it specific about who we serve and what we deliver?
- Could a new employee, given the mission, predict the day-to-day work?
- Is it action-oriented (we do X for Y)?

**Examples:**
- "Provide cloud infrastructure that makes building software 10x easier for small developers."
- "Manage retirement savings for working Swiss professionals so they can stop worrying about money."

## Vision

**What it is:** Where the company is going. The future state we are working toward.

**Time horizon:** 5–10 year horizon.

**Beneficiary:** Internal — orients the organization toward a shared future.

**Tests:**
- Is it ambitious but plausible?
- Does it describe a future state, not a current one?
- Does it shape investment and capability decisions today?

**Examples:**
- "By 2030, the leading provider of [X] in [region/segment]."
- "A company where every product visibly extends the user's capability."

## Values

**What it is:** How we behave. The principles that guide how decisions are made and how people work together.

**Time horizon:** Long-lived, deeply embedded.

**Beneficiary:** Internal culture and external trust.

**Tests:**
- Are they specific enough to guide a difficult decision?
- Do they identify behaviors we explicitly will not tolerate?
- Are they lived, not just printed?

**Examples (illustrative):**
- "Honesty over comfort." (We say the hard thing rather than the polite thing.)
- "Customer outcome over revenue protection." (When the two conflict, we choose the outcome.)

## How they fit together

A coherent framework:

```
PURPOSE (Why we exist)
   ↓
VISION (Where we are going)
   ↓
MISSION (What we do now)
   ↓
VALUES (How we behave)
```

Purpose is the durable anchor. Vision is the directional pull. Mission is the operational focus. Values are the behavioral discipline.

## Common confusions

### Mission mistaken for purpose
"Our mission is to be the leading provider of X." That is a vision (or a pretentious commercial claim). Not a purpose.

### Vision mistaken for purpose
"Our purpose is to be number 1 in our market by 2030." That is a goal. Not a purpose.

### Values list as purpose substitute
A long values list without an integrating purpose. Reads as compliance language.

### Purpose as ad copy
Purpose statements written by marketing for marketing. Smooth, generic, untethered from the business.

## What good looks like

A company with a clear framework can produce a one-paragraph statement that connects all four:

> "We exist to [purpose]. We pursue this by [mission], working toward [vision], honoring [values] in everything we do."

The paragraph should sound like *this* company, not like a generic template. The test: rewrite the paragraph with a competitor's name. If it still reads true, the framework is too generic.

## When to revise each

Different cadences:

- **Purpose** — rarely; only when fundamental contribution shifts. Decades.
- **Vision** — every 5–10 years; align with strategy cycles.
- **Mission** — every 3–5 years; align with portfolio shifts.
- **Values** — review every 2–3 years; refine but rarely overhaul.

Pages 100 of a strategy review should not refresh the purpose. The instability disqualifies it from being a purpose.

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 1 of the SKILL.md workflow, audit what the user's company has. Often the existing artifact labeled "purpose" is actually a mission or a vision; what is called "mission" is actually a strategy. Reclassify before doing new work. Sometimes the existing artifacts are fine — they are just labeled wrong.
