# Signature Programs — Aaker's Operational Expression of Purpose

David Aaker's central contribution: purpose is not a slogan but a set of sustained, identifiable programs that make the purpose visible in the world. Without signature programs, a stated purpose is wallpaper.

## What a signature program is

A signature program is:

1. **Sustained** — multi-year, ideally multi-decade. Not a campaign.
2. **Identifiable** — has a name, a face, a place in the world. People can point to it.
3. **Owned** — the company is the author and operator, not just a sponsor or partner.
4. **Aligned** — visibly connected to the company's stated purpose.
5. **Impactful** — produces real outcomes (commercial, social, environmental), not just visibility.
6. **Connected to the business** — relates to what the company actually does, not a charity side project.

## Classic examples

### Dove — "Real Beauty"
Started in 2004. Sustained for 20+ years. Identifiable globally. Owned by Dove. Aligned with the purpose of "challenging narrow beauty standards." Measurable impact on category dynamics and brand strength.

### Patagonia — Worn Wear, Footprint Chronicles, "Don't Buy This Jacket"
Multiple sustained programs. Each identifiable. Each clearly owned by Patagonia. Aligned with environmental purpose. Generates real environmental and commercial outcomes.

### Microsoft — Accessibility
Inclusive Design, the Xbox Adaptive Controller, accessibility commitments across Office and Windows. Sustained over years. Identifiable. Owned by Microsoft. Aligned with the inclusion purpose. Impactful for the disabled community and a meaningful business advantage in enterprise sales.

### LEGO — Education and creativity programs
LEGO Education, LEGO Foundation, Build the Change. Sustained. Identifiable. Aligned with creativity/learning purpose. Connects to the core business.

### Nike — Equity initiatives
Black Community Commitment, Made to Play. Both sustained. Identifiable. Aligned with the broader "inspire athletes" purpose extended into access and equity.

## Anti-examples — what is NOT a signature program

- **One-off campaigns.** Lasted 6 months. Not sustained.
- **Sponsorships.** Wrote a check to an organization doing the work. Not owned.
- **Generic CSR.** Funded local food banks. Worthy but not signature — disconnected from business and unspecific to the company.
- **Marketing ad with a cause.** A commercial about a value. Not a program.

Many companies confuse these with signature programs and wonder why their purpose isn't landing.

## The Aaker test for a signature program

A program is signature if it passes:

### 1. Stick test
Has it been sustained for 3+ years and committed for 3+ more? If not, it is a campaign or a pilot.

### 2. Name test
Does it have its own name and identity? Can people refer to it?

### 3. Connection test
Does it connect visibly to the company's purpose and business?

### 4. Impact test
Does it produce real outcomes that can be evidenced? Not just impressions and engagement — actual change.

### 5. Authenticity test
Could a journalist trace the program's claims to operating behavior and find them substantiated?

## Designing a new signature program

A working sequence:

### Step 1 — Connect to purpose
Which dimension of the company's purpose is this program expressing? Be specific. If multiple, pick the primary.

### Step 2 — Identify the contribution
What specific contribution does the program make? Whose lives change? In what way?

### Step 3 — Identify the business connection
How does the program connect to the company's products, services, or capabilities? A program disconnected from the business is harder to sustain and less authentic.

### Step 4 — Design the form
- Recurring product (e.g., Patagonia's Worn Wear repair program)
- Annual event or initiative
- Partnership program with specific structure
- Platform or marketplace
- Sustained content series (educational, journalism, advocacy)
- Internal program that becomes external visible

### Step 5 — Commit the resources
- Multi-year budget envelope
- Senior owner with named accountability
- Team dedicated, not borrowed
- Reporting and measurement designed in

### Step 6 — Build the measurement
- Output metrics (what we did)
- Outcome metrics (what changed for beneficiaries)
- Business metrics (brand, customer, recruitment, etc.)
- Quality metrics (substance, not just volume)

### Step 7 — Sustain
- Annual review and refresh
- Resist the urge to refresh-rebrand every cycle (the sustained identity is the point)
- Communication that compounds the program's recognition

## Common failures

- **Underfunded.** Announced with fanfare; resourced like a side project. Predictably stalls.
- **Owned by communications only.** Marketing teams driving a program that requires operating commitment from elsewhere. Friction; eventually decay.
- **Disconnected from business.** Worthy social cause; no organic connection to what the company does. Reads as donation rather than commitment.
- **Refreshed too often.** New name every two years. Never builds recognition.
- **Output-focused only.** Measures activities, not outcomes. Volume without substance.

## Portfolio thinking

A mature purpose-driven company typically has 2–4 signature programs at any given time, plus a portfolio of supporting initiatives. The signature programs are the public face; the supporting initiatives are the operational depth.

Less than 2 signature programs and the purpose is anchored on too thin a base. More than 4 and the visibility dilutes.

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 4 of the SKILL.md workflow:

1. Audit existing programs against the Aaker test
2. Identify which (if any) currently qualify as signature programs
3. Design new programs or upgrade existing ones to signature standard
4. Build the measurement and the sustained commitment

A purpose statement without signature programs is wishful thinking. With them, the purpose has a body in the world.
