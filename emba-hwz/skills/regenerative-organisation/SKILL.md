# Zielbild der re-generativen Organisation — Reimagining What a Company Is For

This skill operationalizes EMBA Block 4 Tag 1 (Prof. Antoinette Weibel & Pascal Nonnen, HWZ). Its premise is critical and uncomfortable: most contemporary corporate failures (Credit Suisse, Wirecard, Boeing 737 MAX, Volkswagen diesel, scandal-rich pharmaceuticals) are not bad-apple problems. They are operating-system problems. The company-as-machine, profit-maximization-as-purpose model produces predictable patterns of misconduct because it suppresses the moral and adaptive capacities the company actually needs.

The work of this skill is to help leaders develop a *Zielbild* — a target picture — of a company that produces sustainable prosperity for its stakeholders, its people, and society. Not idealism: a clear-eyed redesign of purpose, work, accountability, and structure.

## When to lean on this skill

Use it when the user is: defining or revisiting company purpose, dealing with a values gap between stated ideals and lived reality, designing post-shareholder-primacy governance, redesigning compensation and incentive structures, addressing a scandal or culture crisis, defining "good work" for their context, building leadership accountability beyond financial KPIs, or developing the integrating narrative for a transformation.

Do not use it for: tactical organizational design questions (different toolkit), compensation policy details (HR specialist), or compliance fixes (governance specialist).

## Mental model: from machine to organism

The company-as-machine model treats the organization as an instrumental device: inputs in (capital, labor), output out (returns). Managers are mechanics. Employees are interchangeable parts. Purpose is profit. Externalities are someone else's problem.

The regenerative organization model treats the company as a living system embedded in larger systems (society, ecology). It generates value through, and for, multiple stakeholders. It has obligations, not just objectives. Its purpose runs from inside-out — what we are *for*, not just what we are *good at*.

Two corollaries to hold throughout:

1. **The machine model is not wrong — it is incomplete.** Mechanical clarity matters. Process discipline matters. The mistake is to treat the machine model as the whole truth.
2. **Regenerative is not a synonym for soft.** A genuinely regenerative organization is more demanding, not less. It requires honesty, accountability, voice, and the willingness to be measured by harder standards.

## Workflow

### 1. Locate the user's organization

Read `references/the-cockpit.md`. Use the cockpit framework to diagnose where the user's organization sits today across the demands modern stakeholders place on it:

- **Stakeholder expectations** — what shareholders, customers, employees, society, partners actually expect
- **Behaviors observed** — what the company actually does
- **The gaps** — between aspiration and behavior, between behavior and consequence
- **The narrative the company tells about itself** — and whether it survives contact with the evidence

Where the gaps are large, name them honestly. This is the diagnostic step. Avoid skipping to solutions.

### 2. Diagnose the operating logic

Read `references/limitations-of-the-machine.md`. Explicitly diagnose which features of the machine model are creating the patterns the user is seeing. Common patterns:

- Accountability that flows up the hierarchy but not out to stakeholders
- Compensation that rewards behaviors the values statement says are wrong
- Risk systems that protect short-term P&L while accumulating long-term liabilities
- Speak-up cultures that exist on paper but not in practice
- Performance management that pits individuals against each other

Each pattern has a structural cause. Naming the cause is the unlock for redesign.

### 3. Develop the inside-out purpose

Read `references/inside-out-purpose.md`. Most corporate purpose statements are outside-in: written for external audiences, marketing-grade, aspirational. They fail to anchor decisions because no one inside actually believes they shape behavior.

An *inside-out purpose*:

- Is something the company is already partly doing, recognizable to employees
- States the contribution the company makes that the world would notice if it disappeared
- Names the trade-offs the company is willing to make to honor it
- Holds even when honoring it costs profit

Use `templates/inside-out-purpose-canvas.md`. The test: would a frontline employee, given the purpose statement and an awkward decision, find guidance in it?

### 4. Define what "good" means here

The Weibel/Nonnen provocation: most management thought presupposes a definition of "good" — usually "good is what produces profit" — and never interrogates it. The work is to develop an explicit, context-specific definition.

For each of the three domains, develop a working definition:

- **Good work** — what does excellent work look like here, beyond meeting targets? Work that builds capability, dignity, contribution. See `references/good-work.md`.
- **Good leadership** — what does responsible leadership look like, beyond delivering numbers? Accountability to multiple stakeholders, regulation of self-interest, willingness to lose status to maintain integrity.
- **Good products and services** — what does excellence look like, beyond customer satisfaction surveys? Products that produce net positive value over their full life cycle.

These definitions then become the standards against which the organization is built.

### 5. Design the regenerative structure — the atmende Verfassung

Read `references/atmende-verfassung.md`. The "breathing constitution" idea: an organization's governance, incentives, voice mechanisms, and accountability structures need to be alive — capable of breathing with the world's changes, not frozen in a particular legal or organizational form.

Practical components:

- **Stakeholder voice mechanisms** — formal channels for employees, customers, communities, suppliers to influence decisions
- **Compensation and KPI design** — balanced across stakeholders, with the willingness to make trade-offs visible
- **Governance with accountability beyond shareholders** — board composition, fiduciary duties, escalation paths
- **Risk and ethics infrastructure** — that surfaces problems before they become crises
- **Decision rights that match decisions** — not all decisions belong at the top; not all belong on the floor

### 6. Address the work — gemeinsam Wert schaffen

Work in a regenerative organization is not just "fulfillment" or "engagement." It is the collaborative creation of value for the multiple stakeholders the company serves. This frames:

- Why work is done (purpose-linked)
- How work is done (capability-building, dignified, social)
- Who decides about work (subsidiarity — decisions at the level that has the relevant knowledge)
- How contribution is measured (multi-stakeholder value, not just individual output)

### 7. Build the integrating narrative

Use `templates/regenerative-organisation-narrative.md`. The narrative ties together purpose, definitions of good, structure, and work into a single story the organization can carry. The test: would the narrative survive both an idealist's scrutiny ("is this honest?") and a cynic's scrutiny ("does this actually change anything?").

## Output style

Honest, substantive, willing to name uncomfortable truths. Avoid both the cynical mode ("companies just want to make money, all else is theatre") and the naive mode ("if we just write a beautiful purpose statement, we are good"). The truth is harder: making this work requires structural change, not rhetoric.

Use specific examples — including failures (Credit Suisse, Wirecard, Boeing) — to ground the conversation. Avoid abstract values discourse without operational consequence.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- **Purpose theater.** Beautiful statement, no behavioral consequence. The Credit Suisse "Code of Conduct" was eloquent; the behavior was not.
- **Greenwashing 2.0 — "regenerative" as marketing.** The term is appropriating. Use it when the structural commitments are actually present.
- **Stakeholder-everything paralysis.** Trying to serve every stakeholder equally produces no priorities. The work is to define the trade-offs and the priorities, honestly.
- **Soft regenerative.** Regenerative organizations are more demanding, not less. Anyone selling this as comfortable transformation is selling something else.
- **Top-down only.** Regenerative structure can only be built with widespread voice. The senior team alone cannot decide what good work means.
- **Confusing co-determination with co-decision.** Voice does not always mean veto. Designing voice rights without designing decision rights produces drift.
