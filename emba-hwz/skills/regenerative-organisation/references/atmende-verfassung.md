# The Breathing Constitution — Living Governance for a Regenerative Organization

"Atmende Verfassung" — the breathing constitution. Weibel and Nonnen's term for the governance and structural design that allows an organization to remain alive: responsive, adaptive, accountable to its multiple stakeholders, capable of changing as the world changes.

The metaphor matters. A breathing constitution moves. It takes in feedback (oxygen); it releases what is no longer useful (carbon dioxide). A frozen constitution suffocates the organization.

## What needs to breathe

Five structural features need to be alive, not fixed:

### 1. Purpose
Stated, but revisited regularly. The purpose serves the organization; the organization does not serve the literal text of a purpose statement frozen in a 1990s brand workshop.

### 2. Governance
Board composition, fiduciary duties, decision rights — designed to allow new voices in as the company changes and as expectations of business change.

### 3. Compensation and KPIs
The metrics that shape behavior must evolve as what matters evolves. A compensation system rewarding 2010 behaviors in 2025 produces 2010 behaviors.

### 4. Voice mechanisms
The channels through which employees, customers, communities feed back. They must work today, not have worked in a survey three years ago.

### 5. Risk and integrity infrastructure
What is flagged, what is escalated, what is acted on. The infrastructure must be calibrated to the actual current risks, not the ones that mattered when it was designed.

## The principles

Five operating principles for a breathing constitution:

### Principle 1 — Subsidiarity
Decisions are made at the level that has the knowledge and bears the consequence. Strategic decisions at the top; operational decisions where the work happens; ethical decisions everywhere. Subsidiarity is the opposite of micromanagement; it is also the opposite of executive abdication.

### Principle 2 — Voice as structural, not granted
Voice mechanisms are designed into the structure, not extended as favor. Permanent ones: works councils where applicable, employee surveys with consequences, customer councils, supplier feedback channels. Episodic ones: open consultation periods on major decisions.

### Principle 3 — Trade-offs made visible
A breathing constitution acknowledges trade-offs explicitly. "We are pursuing X over Y for these reasons; here is what we are willing to give up." The trade-offs are documented and revisited.

### Principle 4 — Time horizons matched to issues
Climate is a 30-year horizon. Talent is a career horizon. R&D is a 5–10 year horizon. Each decision should be made with the time horizon that matches the underlying reality, not collapsed to quarterly.

### Principle 5 — Power flows with information
Information about the company concentrates with senior leadership. Information about the work concentrates with the people doing it. A breathing constitution moves power to where the information is, not asks information to come to where the power is.

## Practical components

### Governance
- **Board composition** — beyond financial expertise; including sustainability, employee voice, technology, ethics
- **Fiduciary framing** — including under company law where stakeholder consideration is explicit (UK Companies Act §172; German Aktiengesetz; emerging frameworks)
- **Stakeholder advisory bodies** — formal, with charters and authority, not decorative
- **Independent challenge** — non-executive directors with genuine independence; audit and risk committees with teeth

### Compensation and KPIs
- **Balanced scorecards** that genuinely include non-financial dimensions, with weight that matters
- **Long-term components** with vesting tied to behaviors over years, not quarters
- **Stakeholder metrics** in senior incentive design — customer outcomes, employee outcomes, environmental, governance
- **Clawback and malus** provisions that operate when behavior turns out to have been damaging
- **Caps on internal pay ratios** — explicit boundaries on inequality within the firm

### Voice
- **Employee voice** — works councils, regular structured surveys, town halls with real questions, anonymized speak-up
- **Customer voice** — beyond NPS, structured advisory councils, complaints handled at senior level
- **Supplier voice** — feedback on the company as a buyer, complaint channels, fair payment practices
- **Community voice** — for facility-based companies, regular structured engagement with affected communities
- **Whistleblower protection** — real, including against retaliation

### Risk and integrity
- **Risk dashboards** that include reputational, regulatory, employee, environmental, not just financial
- **Speak-up channels** that work — measured by usage, by the gap between issue and disclosure, by retaliation incidents
- **Ethics committee** with senior authority and independence
- **Periodic culture audits** — not engagement surveys but the deeper "what kind of company is this?" question

### Adaptive practices
- **Annual constitution review** — what should breathe in or out
- **Sunset clauses** on policies and structures — re-justification rather than indefinite continuation
- **Internal mobility** — preventing role calcification
- **External challenge** — invited critics, independent reviews, board questioning

## The Credit Suisse counterexample

Reverse the principles to see how they would have applied:

- **Subsidiarity:** Risk decisions belonged with risk people who knew. Instead, business heads overrode risk.
- **Voice:** Internal critics existed; they were marginalized.
- **Trade-offs visible:** The trade-offs (revenue against risk control) were not explicit; revenue won implicitly.
- **Time horizons:** Quarterly bonus pressure trumped longer-term risk.
- **Power with information:** The relevant information was at the operating level; the power was at the deal level; the connection was broken.

Each principle would have flagged the trouble earlier.

## The leadership requirement

A breathing constitution requires leadership willing to be measured by it. Specifically:

- Senior leaders accept that they are part of the system that has to change
- Senior leaders accept being subject to the voice mechanisms they install
- Senior leaders accept compensation design that ties their reward to the multi-stakeholder dimensions
- Senior leaders accept the slower pace of decisions that real voice produces, because the decisions are more durable

If these are not present, the constitution is decorative.

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 5 of the SKILL.md workflow, design the breathing constitution components for the user's organization. Be specific. Recommend changes in compensation, in board composition, in voice mechanisms, in time horizons. Most companies will recognize the gap between the principles and the current reality immediately; the work is the structural changes that close it.
