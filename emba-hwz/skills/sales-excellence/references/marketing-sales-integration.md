# Marketing and Sales — Integration vs. Cooperation

In most B2B companies, marketing and sales are two organizations sharing a customer. The customer experiences the fragmentation. The companies experience the conversion gaps. Real integration is structural, not relational.

## Why integration matters

For B2B specifically:

- Customers research 70%+ of the purchase before talking to sales
- Buying committees average 6+ stakeholders
- Sales cycle includes multiple touchpoints across marketing and sales channels
- Marketing-generated leads convert vastly differently from outbound sales

If marketing and sales are not integrated, the same customer gets contradictory messages, the same lead gets disputed, the same opportunity wastes effort across both functions.

## The five layers of integration

### Layer 1 — Shared customer definition

Both functions agree, in writing, on:

- **Who is the target customer?** Specific firmographic, demographic, behavioral criteria.
- **What is the ideal customer profile (ICP)?** With characteristics that predict success.
- **Who is not the target?** Anti-ICP equally important.
- **What is the buying committee makeup?** Roles, decision authority, influence.

Without this, marketing generates leads sales considers unqualified, and sales pursues leads marketing didn't generate.

### Layer 2 — Shared funnel

Joint ownership of the funnel from first touch to closed-won and beyond:

- Marketing owns top of funnel and nurture, but is accountable for opportunity creation, not just lead volume
- Sales owns opportunity-to-close, but is accountable for use of marketing assets and feedback
- Customer success owns retention and expansion, but is accountable to marketing for advocacy

The hand-offs are explicit; the cumulative outcome is shared.

### Layer 3 — Shared metrics

Both functions share, partially, on:

- **Pipeline contribution** by source (marketing-sourced vs. sales-sourced)
- **Conversion at each stage**
- **Customer acquisition cost** and time-to-payback
- **Customer lifetime value** vs. acquisition cost

Pure-function metrics (MQLs only for marketing, closed-revenue only for sales) produce blame culture. Shared metrics produce shared accountability.

### Layer 4 — Shared data

Single customer view across:

- Marketing automation
- CRM
- Customer success / support
- Product usage (if SaaS)
- Billing

Without unified data, the two functions reason from different facts.

### Layer 5 — Shared cadence

Operating rhythm:

- **Weekly:** marketing-sales sync on leads, opportunities, pipeline
- **Monthly:** joint review of conversion, content effectiveness, campaign ROI
- **Quarterly:** joint planning, including content/campaign and outbound coordination
- **Annually:** joint strategy refresh with shared goals

## Account-Based Marketing (ABM) as integration

ABM is the most developed form of marketing-sales integration:

- Marketing and sales jointly define target accounts
- Personalized engagement across multiple stakeholders within each account
- Joint pursuit teams for high-value accounts
- Metrics at account level, not just lead level
- Long horizon (6–18 month account engagement)

ABM works when both functions actually operate together. It is theater when "ABM" is just retargeting ads at named accounts without sales engagement.

## The common failure patterns

### Lead quality wars
Marketing claims to deliver volume. Sales rejects most leads. Marketing claims sales doesn't follow up. Mutual blame. Both partially right.

**Fix:** shared lead definition with both qualitative and behavioral criteria. Documented hand-off SLAs. Joint review of rejected leads.

### MQL-as-marketing-end-state
Marketing focuses on producing MQLs. Marketing performance evaluated on MQL volume. Marketing has no incentive to care about what happens after.

**Fix:** marketing metrics include downstream conversion (MQL→SQL→opportunity→close). Comp partially on shared funnel.

### Content disconnected from sales reality
Marketing produces content based on its own ideas about customer interests. Sales has no input. Content goes unused in actual sales conversations.

**Fix:** content development informed by sales' actual experience. Sales involved in content review. Content effectiveness measured by use in deals.

### Outbound and inbound disconnect
Sales runs outbound prospecting. Marketing runs inbound generation. Each operates blind to the other. Same accounts get hit from both directions inconsistently.

**Fix:** integrated cadence at account level. Marketing nurture coordinated with sales outbound. ABM as the integrating framework.

### Different customer views
Marketing uses one definition; sales another. Customer success a third. The "same" customer means different things in each system.

**Fix:** unified customer master data. Single source of truth. Governance over who can change customer definitions.

## What good integration looks like

Visible signs:

- One quarterly business review for both functions, run jointly
- Marketing and sales share an executive (CMO + CRO often into a CCO, or formal integration mechanisms)
- Sellers know what marketing is running this quarter and why; marketers can name the top 10 deals in pipeline
- Customer success input feeds marketing campaigns and sales playbooks
- Single forecast that includes marketing pipeline contribution
- Comp design that includes some shared components

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 5 of the SKILL.md workflow:

1. Assess the current state across the five layers
2. Identify which layer is the binding constraint
3. Recommend specific structural moves (not "more meetings between marketing and sales")
4. Build the integration roadmap over 12–18 months
5. Anchor on shared customer definition (Layer 1) — without that, the others cannot be built

Marketing-sales integration is one of the most common high-ROI moves in B2B. Most companies underinvest in it.
