# Sales Excellence Maturity — A Five-Dimension Framework

Drawn from the Staudacher / CustomersX work and the Sales Excellence Study (2022). A diagnostic that surfaces where a company actually stands — not where its slide deck says it stands.

## The five dimensions, each scored 1–5

### Dimension 1 — Customer-oriented company design

- **Level 1:** Organization built around products or internal functions. Customer is downstream of internal processes.
- **Level 2:** Customer mentioned in strategy. Some attempts at customer journey mapping. No structural change.
- **Level 3:** Some functions reorganized around customer outcomes. Mixed signals.
- **Level 4:** Customer outcomes drive structure, metrics, and decision rights for most of the org.
- **Level 5:** Genuinely customer-oriented across product, sales, marketing, success, support. End-to-end ownership clear.

### Dimension 2 — Sales process systematization

- **Level 1:** No defined process. Each seller does what they think works.
- **Level 2:** Process documented but inconsistently followed. Stage definitions vary.
- **Level 3:** Process defined and trained. Some instrumentation in CRM. Adoption uneven.
- **Level 4:** Process used by most sellers, with regular coaching against it. Performance data feeds improvement.
- **Level 5:** Process is a living system, refined continuously, with mature playbooks per buyer type and deal pattern.

### Dimension 3 — Marketing-sales integration

- **Level 1:** Separate functions, separate metrics, blame culture at handoff.
- **Level 2:** Joint planning sessions; shared targets at top level; operational independence.
- **Level 3:** Some shared metrics (MQL→SQL→pipeline). Account-based pilots in places.
- **Level 4:** Shared customer definition, shared funnel ownership, joint compensation on customer outcomes for key roles.
- **Level 5:** Functions operate as one customer-facing system. Joint planning, joint review, joint accountability for full funnel.

### Dimension 4 — Customer data management

- **Level 1:** Fragmented data across systems. Quality issues unknown or unaddressed.
- **Level 2:** Core systems consolidated; CRM is partial; data quality variable.
- **Level 3:** Single customer view starting to emerge. Behavioral and value data partial.
- **Level 4:** Mature unified profile; quality monitored; usable for decisions.
- **Level 5:** Data products with SLAs; customer insight is a strategic capability; predictive use is routine.

### Dimension 5 — Sales tech and AI maturity

- **Level 1:** CRM exists but is for reporting only; sellers don't use it. No meaningful AI use.
- **Level 2:** CRM partially adopted. Some marketing automation. Vendor pitches drive AI tool adoption without integration.
- **Level 3:** Sales tech stack is rationalized; key tools are actually used; some AI applications produce real value.
- **Level 4:** Tech stack supports the process; AI applications integrated into seller workflow; measured value.
- **Level 5:** Tech and AI are differentiators; co-pilot, analytics, automation work together to multiply seller capability.

## Reading the pattern

Most B2B companies score 2.5–3.5 average across these dimensions, often higher on Dimension 5 (because they have bought the software) and much lower on Dimensions 2 and 4 (because the process and data don't support the software).

The binding constraint is usually the lowest dimension. Investing in Dimension 5 while Dimensions 2 and 4 are at level 2 produces low return — the new tools sit on top of unsystematic process and fragmented data.

## What each score level implies for action

### Average ≤ 2
The work is foundational. Define the sales process. Build basic customer data hygiene. Don't buy more software. Develop sales management capability.

### Average 2–3
Systematize. Build the instrumentation. Train the process. Begin marketing-sales integration. Start consolidating data.

### Average 3–4
Scale. Mature the process. Embed AI in specific high-value applications. Build data products. Push integration of marketing and sales further.

### Average ≥ 4
Differentiate. Sales capability becomes a competitive advantage. Invest in proprietary data and AI capabilities.

## Common diagnostic patterns

### "We have CRM but it's a data graveyard"
Dimension 5 at 3 (technology exists), Dimensions 2 and 4 at 1–2 (process and data don't support it). Common in companies that rushed to Salesforce / HubSpot without process work.

### "Marketing generates leads but sales doesn't close them"
Dimension 3 at 1–2 (no real integration). Lead quality issues, definition mismatches, handoff frictions. Universal in B2B before serious integration work.

### "We don't know which customers are profitable"
Dimension 4 at 1–2 (unified customer value data missing). Operates on intuition; cross-sell and upsell decisions are gut-based.

### "Sales productivity has plateaued or declined"
Often Dimension 2 issue (process unsystematic) plus Dimension 5 issue (tools don't help sellers). Sometimes Dimension 1 issue (organization fights against customer outcomes).

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 1 of the SKILL.md workflow, run the diagnostic honestly. Identify the binding constraint. Resist the urge to attack all five dimensions simultaneously — sequencing matters. Typically:

1. Process (Dimension 2) first — gives the rest something to hang on
2. Data (Dimension 4) in parallel — informs everything else
3. Integration (Dimension 3) next — once data and process exist, the integration has substance
4. Customer orientation (Dimension 1) is the longest arc — structural change, takes 2–3 years
5. Tech/AI (Dimension 5) earns its keep once the others are at level 3+
