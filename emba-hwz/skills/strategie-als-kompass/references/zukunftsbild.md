# The Zukunftsbild — Future Image as Strategic Compass

The Zukunftsbild (future image) is the central artifact of this strategy approach. Distinct from both a vision statement (too abstract) and a multi-year plan (too specific), it is an evocative but concrete description of *what the company will look and feel like* if the strategy works.

## Why Zukunftsbild instead of vision statement

The typical vision statement — "to be the world's leading X" — does no work. It does not help anyone make a decision, because every decision in the organization can be defended as somehow contributing to "being the leading X."

The Zukunftsbild's job is to be *specific enough that some choices become obviously inconsistent with it*. If the Zukunftsbild does not rule anything out, it is not pulling its weight.

## What a good Zukunftsbild contains

Six dimensions, all populated with enough specificity to be useful:

### 1. What we will be known for
Not "great quality" or "innovative" — the specific reputation the company is building toward. Concretely: when people in our industry describe us in 2030, what words and stories do they use?

### 2. Who we serve and how
Which customer segments are we deliberately for, which are we deliberately not for? What does serving them excellently look like at the moment of value exchange?

### 3. What our work will look like for employees
What is the texture of work? What does a typical week of an engineer / salesperson / customer success rep look like? What kind of people will thrive here? What kinds of people won't?

### 4. What our economic engine will look like
Where the revenue comes from, what the cost structure is, what the unit economics look like, what is the company's competitive moat. Not a financial plan — the shape of the business.

### 5. What we will have stopped doing
This is the line that makes a Zukunftsbild credible. What products, customer segments, geographies, practices will we have shed by 2030? If the answer is "everything we do today, but more," the Zukunftsbild is wishful thinking.

### 6. The personality and identity of the company
The culture, the values lived through behavior, the kind of company someone is proud to work for and recommend. Not a poster — the felt experience.

## Process for creating a Zukunftsbild

It is co-created, not declared. Typical sequence:

### Phase 1 — Inputs
- Status quo analysis (what is real today)
- Environmental observation (what is moving)
- Stakeholder interviews (employees, customers, partners, board)
- Trend and scenario inputs from the `zukunftsforschung` work

### Phase 2 — First draft
- Senior leadership team workshop, typically 1–2 days
- Draft populated across the six dimensions
- Tested for specificity: does it rule things out?

### Phase 3 — Engagement
- Broader leadership group review and refinement
- Selected operational level engagement — not for redrafting, for testing whether it lands
- Customer advisory input where appropriate

### Phase 4 — Articulation
- A short written version (1 page) for executive use
- An evocative narrative version (~3 pages) for broader sharing
- Selected stories and examples to make it concrete
- Visualizations where helpful (NOT corporate stock photography — actual depictions)

### Phase 5 — Commitment
- Executive team signs on to use it as the anchor for strategic decisions
- The 3–5 strategic objectives are derived from it
- The rhythm of revisiting (annually + triggered) is set

## What a Zukunftsbild is not

- **A prediction.** It is what the company is committing to *build*, not forecasting the world will give us.
- **A vision statement.** Far more specific.
- **A plan.** Does not specify how — that is the strategy work that flows from it.
- **A marketing artifact.** Internal first. Marketing can come later.
- **Permanent.** Refreshed periodically, especially when fundamentals shift.

## Common failure modes

- **Too generic.** Could apply to any company. Useless.
- **Photogenic but unfunded.** Beautiful but not connected to the resource and capability picture.
- **Unilateral.** Created by 3 people in a room, then "communicated." Does not land.
- **Frozen.** Once written, never revisited. Eventually becomes wallpaper.
- **Contradicts the culture's actual aspiration.** A Zukunftsbild only the executive team wants will not pull the organization.

## Diagnostic — is your Zukunftsbild doing its job?

- Can you point to a recent decision where the Zukunftsbild meaningfully shaped the choice?
- Can you point to something the company explicitly decided not to do because it was inconsistent with the Zukunftsbild?
- Do senior managers below the C-suite refer to the Zukunftsbild in their own decision making, unprompted?
- Could a new hire, given the Zukunftsbild and the status quo, predict what kind of work the company would prioritize?

If any answer is no, the Zukunftsbild is either too vague, not yet owned, or not yet operationalized.

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 4 of the SKILL.md workflow, help the user produce a Zukunftsbild that passes the diagnostic. Iterate. A first draft is rarely sharp enough — the second and third drafts are where it becomes a compass.
