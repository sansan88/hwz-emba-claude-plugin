# Megatrends — Swiss & Global Catalog

This reference distills the Roos / swissfuture megatrend framework taught in B1T1 and pairs it with the STEEP+L structure for systematic scanning. Use it to populate the trend landscape in step 2 of the workflow.

## STEEP+L scanning grid

Use the grid as a forcing function — go through each cell and ask "what is changing here for this user's context?". Trends that don't make any cell interesting are noise.

## Swiss core megatrends (Roos 2018, condensed and updated)

The original swissfuture report identified eight clusters. The framing below keeps the spirit but expresses each cluster in terms relevant for executive decisions in 2025+.

### 1. Demographics & life stages
Aging population, longer working lives, smaller cohorts, migration as labor supply. Implications: workforce planning, product mix shifts toward 60+, succession risk.

### 2. Individualization & values pluralism
The "Wertewandel in der Schweiz 2030" report mapped four value scenarios. Today's reality blends fragments of all four: meaning-seeking, security-seeking, autonomy-seeking, and community-seeking customers and employees coexist. Implications: brand and employee-value-proposition must speak to plural value sets, not assume one.

### 3. Digitalization & AI as ambient infrastructure
The trend graduated from "digital transformation projects" to AI being a substrate of operations. Implications: capability gap is the binding constraint; data architecture and human-AI workflow design are now strategic.

### 4. Ecological transition
From compliance to operating constraint. Carbon pricing, supply chain decarbonization, water and resource limits. Implications: cost curve reshuffling, asset stranding risk, new value pools (circularity, climate tech).

### 5. New mobility & spatial restructuring
Hybrid work, urban-rural rebalancing, mobility-as-service, electrification. Implications: real estate footprint, commute assumptions, location of talent.

### 6. Health & wellbeing
Longer healthspan ambition, mental health visibility, prevention paradigm, digital health. Implications: benefits design, productivity, B2C health-adjacent value pools.

### 7. Geopolitics & deglobalization friction
End of frictionless globalization. Friend-shoring, dual-use export controls, sanctions complexity. Implications: supply chain redesign, market access risk, scenario planning around Taiwan / Ukraine / Middle East tail events.

### 8. Trust, institutions, and information environment
Declining trust in institutions, media fragmentation, deepfake economy, polarization. Implications: corporate communications, regulatory legitimacy, social license to operate.

## How to use this catalog

Do not paste these eight clusters as a result. They are scanning prompts. For each one, ask three questions:

1. **Concretization** — what does this trend look like *in the user's specific context* (industry, geography, customer segment)? Force specificity.
2. **Direction & speed** — is it accelerating, plateauing, reversing? Time-to-material-impact in years.
3. **Driver vs symptom** — is this a root cause or a manifestation? Root drivers belong in scenarios; symptoms belong in the watch list.

A good trend map for a single strategic question typically contains 6–12 trends, not 30. Forcing function: if you cannot tie a trend to a decision the user is making, drop it.

## Linking trends to scenario axes

After building the trend landscape, identify the two trends that are simultaneously:

- **High impact** for the user's decision
- **High uncertainty** in direction or speed

Those two become the axes of the 2x2 scenario matrix in step 3. The other trends become contextual coloring inside each scenario.

## Updating the catalog

The Roos 2018 report is now several years old. When using this skill, surface what has *shifted* since: AI maturation is faster than 2018 imagined; geopolitical fragmentation is sharper; ecological transition has moved from aspiration to regulation. Note these update-deltas in the trend map so the user sees you are not just reciting a stale report.
