# Hope-Grounded Narrative Template

For the integrating story leaders carry into the organization after a scenario exercise. Built on Krafft's three-component model of hope: goal + pathway + agency, with meaning as the binding element.

## Use this structure

### Part 1 — Honest acknowledgment (60–90 seconds when spoken)

Name what is genuinely hard. Concrete, not abstract. Avoid both catastrophizing and minimizing. The goal is to demonstrate you have looked at the situation clearly.

> _Example structure: "Across the scenarios we just worked through, three things are uncomfortable to look at: [X], [Y], [Z]. They will not go away by ignoring them. Some of them are partly outside our control."_

### Part 2 — The agency frame

Name what we can shape. This is the pathway component of hope. Be specific enough that people see real moves, not slogans.

> _Example structure: "What is in our hands: [A], [B], [C]. These are real levers, not wishful thinking. Here is the logic of how they connect to the futures we mapped."_

### Part 3 — The identity frame

Name who we want to be while navigating this. The meaning component of hope. This is what makes the work feel worth doing even when it is hard.

> _Example structure: "While we do this, we want to be the kind of company that [characteristic 1], that [characteristic 2]. That identity is a choice we keep making. It is also what our customers / employees / partners will remember about us."_

### Part 4 — The first concrete move

Name the next action that proves this narrative is real. Without this, the rest is rhetoric.

> _Example structure: "The first thing that has to be true on Monday is [specific action by specific people by specific date]. That is the demonstration that this is not a slide deck — it is how we are actually going to operate."_

## Drafting principles

- Write it as if you will say it out loud. If a sentence does not survive being spoken to a skeptical 200-person town hall, rewrite it.
- Never claim certainty you do not have. Phrases like "we know how this will go" destroy credibility. Phrases like "we don't know everything, here is how we will move anyway" build it.
- Keep it under 4 minutes spoken (~600 words written). Past that, you have a memo, not a narrative.
- Pressure-test on one skeptic before broadcasting. If it does not survive contact with the sharpest cynic on the team, it will not survive the wider org either.
