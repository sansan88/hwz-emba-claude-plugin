# Circle of Influence for the Transformation Leader

Stephen Covey's distinction, applied to the often-frustrated transformation leader: separate what you worry about from what you can move. Spend your energy on the latter; the former feeds frustration without producing change.

## The two circles

### Circle of Concern
Everything that matters to you about this transformation. Wide, diverse, often overwhelming. Includes:
- Macro forces (industry shifts, regulatory change, economic conditions)
- Internal forces beyond your role (board decisions, CEO behavior, other functions)
- Cultural patterns (decades of organizational history)
- People who are skeptical or resistant
- Politics, history, scars from past initiatives

### Circle of Influence
What you can actually move, given your role, energy, and time. Smaller. Specific. Within reach.

The size of the Circle of Influence depends on the leader's role and the leader's craft. A senior leader has a larger Circle of Influence than a middle manager — but middle managers consistently underestimate their own circle.

## The diagnostic

For the user, run the diagnostic:

|------------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------|

|                              |                          |                        |

Often surfaces:
- A long list of concerns
- A shorter list of items in the Circle of Influence
- A renewed clarity about where energy belongs

## The energy law

Covey's observation: energy invested in the Circle of Influence tends to *grow* the circle. Energy invested in the Circle of Concern (worrying about what you cannot move) tends to *shrink* the circle (because you become known as a complainer, your credibility erodes, your ability to influence diminishes).

This is not advice to ignore the wider context — context is the diagnostic. It is advice about where to spend energy after the diagnostic is done.

## Three practical applications for transformation leaders

### 1. Pick experiments inside the Circle of Influence
The first experiments should be in places where you can actually run them — your team, your function, your decision rights. Don't propose experiments that require permission you don't have, unless you are also prepared to do the work of getting that permission.

### 2. Model the change you want
The single most reliable Circle-of-Influence move: change your own behavior in ways consistent with the transformation. Modeling is visible, doesn't require permission, and is contagious.

### 3. Build the coalition where you have access
Influence works through relationships. Map the people you have meaningful access to. Strengthen those relationships. Build understanding of the transformation in your immediate orbit. Then your orbit becomes a platform for wider influence.

## The expanding circle

The Circle of Influence is not fixed. Specific moves that expand it:

- **Reliability** — doing what you said you would do, consistently. Builds the social capital that translates to influence.
- **Visible competence** — demonstrating skill at the work. Earns respect that opens new doors.
- **Generosity** — helping others succeed without immediate reciprocity. Builds the relationships that extend reach.
- **Honesty** — telling the truth even when it costs. Makes you the person others want in the room.
- **Restraint** — not exhausting credibility on the wrong battles. Saves it for when it matters.

Each of these is a leadership craft, not a personality. Transformation leaders develop them deliberately.

## When the Circle of Influence is too small

Sometimes the diagnostic surfaces that the leader's Circle of Influence is genuinely too small to move the transformation. Honest options:

- **Lower the ambition** of the transformation to fit the available influence
- **Expand the role** through deliberate work over time
- **Build alliances** with people whose circles are different and overlapping
- **Escalate** to leaders whose circles do include what is needed (with proposals, not just problems)
- **Leave** if the role and the ambition cannot be reconciled

Each is honest. The dishonest move is to claim a transformation is happening when the resources, authority, and time do not support it.

## A note on the leader's own contribution to the Circle of Concern

Often, what shows up in the Circle of Concern includes behaviors of others that frustrate the leader. Useful diagnostic: how have you contributed to creating or maintaining the patterns you now find frustrating? Not as self-blame, but as honest accounting. Often the leader is part of the system that produces what they want changed. Naming this expands the Circle of Influence — you can change your own contribution.

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 5 of the SKILL.md workflow:

1. Run the Circle of Influence diagnostic with the user
2. Identify the 3–5 specific moves in the Circle of Influence that would most advance the transformation
3. Design the first experiments within the Circle of Influence
4. Acknowledge what is in the Circle of Concern that is genuinely outside the Circle of Influence — and let it be
5. Plan how the Circle of Influence will be expanded over time
