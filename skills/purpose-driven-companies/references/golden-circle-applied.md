# Sinek's Golden Circle — Applied

Simon Sinek's "Start With Why" thesis: people don't buy what you do, they buy why you do it. The Golden Circle is the operational form — three concentric layers, with Why at the center.

The model has been over-cited and under-applied. This reference makes it operational.

## The three layers

### Why (innermost)
The purpose. The cause. The belief. Why this company exists beyond producing profit.

This is *not* the marketing slogan. It is the deeper belief that motivates everything else. If "Why" sounds the same for any company in the category, it is not yet Why.

### How
The distinctive principles, values, methods that the company believes in. How the company pursues the Why differently from anyone else.

Examples:
- Apple: "By challenging the status quo. By thinking differently. By designing for the human, not for the spec sheet."
- Patagonia: "By using business as a tool for environmental change. By making products that last. By spending profits on activism."

How is the bridge between Why and What. Without it, the connection is invisible.

### What (outermost)
The actual products, services, programs, initiatives. The tangible output of the company.

What is the easiest layer to describe. It is also the layer most companies start communication from. Sinek's argument: that order produces the weakest connection.

## Communication from inside out

The standard order — describe what you do, then how you do it, then maybe why — produces commercial communication that resembles every competitor.

Inside-out:

1. **Why first.** Establish the belief, the cause, the commitment.
2. **How second.** Show the distinctive approach.
3. **What third.** Now the products make sense as expressions of the deeper commitment.

The shift is small in words and large in effect. Compare:

- *Outside-in:* "We make computers. They are beautifully designed, simple to use, and user-friendly. Want to buy one?"
- *Inside-out:* "In everything we do, we believe in challenging the status quo. The way we challenge the status quo is by making our products beautifully designed, simple to use, and user-friendly. We just happen to make great computers. Want to buy one?" (Sinek's classic Apple example.)

The second is more persuasive because it engages identity, not just feature comparison.

## Internal alignment from inside out

The Golden Circle is also a discipline for internal alignment.

### When Why is clear:
- Employees know what kind of decisions the company makes
- Hiring filters can be designed around fit with the Why
- Strategic choices test against "does this align with the Why?"
- Communication coherence emerges across the organization

### When Why is unclear:
- Decisions are made on the surface (revenue, cost) without the deeper criteria
- Hiring drifts toward generic competence
- Strategic options are evaluated only on financial logic
- Communication varies by speaker and audience

## Operationalizing the three layers

For the user's company, complete all three:

### Why
- One sentence that names the belief or cause
- Specific enough to be distinguishing
- Stable across products and time

### How
- 3–5 distinctive principles or methods
- Each one observable in actual behavior
- Each one different from what generic competitors would say

### What
- The 3–5 most important products, services, or programs
- Each one explainable as an expression of the Why through the How

Use `templates/golden-circle-canvas.md` for the structured exercise.

## Stress tests

### The competitor test
Take your Why and put a competitor's name in front of it. If it still reads true, it is not yet your Why.

### The behavior test
Can you point to recent decisions where the Why and How shaped the choice? If everything aligns with the Why in retrospect but nothing actually used it, the Golden Circle is theoretical.

### The "and we just happen to" test
Can you complete the sentence "We do X because we believe Y, and we just happen to..."? If the products feel like *expressions* of the belief rather than *substitutes* for it, the framing is working.

### The recruitment test
Would the Why attract the kind of employees who would amplify it? Or would it attract generic talent who would hollow it out?

## Common failures of the Golden Circle

### The "Why" is a benefit not a belief
"To delight customers" — that's a benefit description, not a Why. Why do you want to delight customers? At what cost? In service of what bigger picture?

### The "How" is generic
"With innovation, quality, and customer focus." Could be any company. Useless.

### The "What" is disconnected
A product portfolio that doesn't read as expressions of the Why. The framework collapses.

### The Why was written by marketing
And never tested against the operating reality. Pretty words; no operating consequence.

## From Sinek to Aaker

Sinek's Golden Circle is the strategic articulation. Aaker's Signature Programs (see `signature-programs.md`) are the operational expression — the sustained programs through which the Why becomes visible in the world. The two complement: Sinek for framing, Aaker for execution.

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 3 of the SKILL.md workflow:

1. Develop or refine all three layers
2. Stress-test against the four tests
3. Identify gaps between stated Why and current behavior
4. Plan the operational moves that would close the gaps — usually via signature programs

The Golden Circle is the diagnostic and the design tool. The signature programs are how it becomes real.
