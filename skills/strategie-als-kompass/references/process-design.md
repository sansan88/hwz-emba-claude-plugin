# Strategy Process Design

The thesis of this module: the process by which strategy gets made is the strategy. Three classical schools dominate, each with its problems in current conditions; the modern adaptive process is the synthesis.

## The three classical paradigms (compressed Mintzberg)

### The planning school
Strategy as a deliberate, top-down, analytical exercise. Months of analysis, an integrated plan, cascade through the organization, execute.

**Strengths:** Discipline, clarity, alignment. Works when the future is predictable.

**Weaknesses:** Fragile to surprise. The plan is wrong the moment reality diverges. Often produces compliance behavior rather than commitment. Misses emergent opportunities.

### The positioning school (Porter et al.)
Strategy as choice of position in the competitive landscape. Five forces, value chain, generic strategies.

**Strengths:** Sharp analytical lens. Good for understanding structural attractiveness.

**Weaknesses:** Static. Assumes industries are well-defined. Underweights organizational dynamics and emergence. Often produces analysis paralysis.

### The emergent / learning school
Strategy as pattern in a stream of decisions, much of which emerges from operational experience rather than top-down design. Mintzberg's later work, also Stacey, Brown & Eisenhardt's "competing on the edge."

**Strengths:** Realistic. Embraces uncertainty. Encourages organizational learning.

**Weaknesses:** Can drift without anchor. Hard to communicate. Risks abdication of leadership.

## The modern adaptive process

Combines deliberate direction-setting with emergent execution. Five design principles:

### 1. Compass over blueprint
A clear directional artifact (the Zukunftsbild and 3–5 strategic objectives) anchors choices. The detailed plan is not the strategy — it is one possible path consistent with the strategy.

### 2. Continuous rhythm over annual event
Strategy is a quarterly conversation, not a yearly retreat. The annual cycle deepens and recommits; quarterly cycles adjust to what is being learned.

### 3. Broad participation over executive monopoly
Strategy is co-created with senior leaders, key middle management, and selected operational voices. The executive team owns the final synthesis, but ownership is shared by the people who have to enact it.

### 4. Outside-in scanning over inside-out planning
Environment observation is a structured ongoing practice. The strategy is updated based on what the world is telling us, not just on what our internal analysis concludes.

### 5. Culture-integrated over culture-blind
Strategy work explicitly considers what the culture will do to the strategy (Drucker: culture eats strategy for breakfast) and what the strategy requires the culture to become.

## A working cadence

For a mid-sized organization:

**Weekly / monthly — at the executive team**
- Decisions are made with reference to the strategic compass
- Drift from the compass is named when it appears

**Quarterly — strategic conversation (~90 min)**
- Signal review: what is changing in the environment, what are we learning internally
- Objective progress: are we moving on the 3–5 strategic objectives
- Trade-off review: where are we struggling to act consistent with the strategy
- Recommitment or pivot

**Annually — strategic deepening (1–2 days)**
- Status quo refresh
- Zukunftsbild touch-up
- Objective re-commitment or replacement
- Engagement plan for the year

**Triennially — strategic refresh (longer cycle)**
- Zukunftsbild renewal
- Possibly major strategy shift
- Broader engagement of the organization

## Process choice depends on context

- **High-volatility industry:** Shorter cycles. Quarterly may be too slow; monthly strategic conversations may be needed.
- **Stable industry:** Longer cycles. Annual depth may be sufficient.
- **Large complex organizations:** More structured engagement layer, more formal communication.
- **Smaller organizations:** Faster, less formal, but the same components must exist.

## Common process failures and their fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Strategy is the executive team's secret | Co-create with senior leaders; engage broadly in implementation |
| Annual offsite produces beautiful deck, no behavior change | Build the quarterly rhythm and the connection to daily decisions |
| Strategy contradicts what the culture allows | Strategy + culture work as one stream, not parallel |
| Strategy is too abstract to act on | Build Zukunftsbild + 3–5 specific objectives |
| Strategy is too specific, becomes obsolete | Compass over blueprint; expect to refresh |
| No mechanism to update | Quarterly cadence with trigger conditions |
| Endless analysis, no decision | Time-box analysis; force choices; commit to learn-by-doing |

## How to use this in the workflow

In step 1 of the SKILL.md workflow, help the user explicitly choose a process design. Many "strategy problems" turn out to be process problems on inspection. Naming the process design and building the rhythm is itself a major contribution.
